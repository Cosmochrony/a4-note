  \begin{abstract}
    This companion of the Projective Residue Schur reduction (PRS) takes up its open deliverable on the Lorentzian
    saturation functional controlling the generation split \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u}.
    The generation modulus is \texorpdfstring{$\JPi$}{J_Π}-odd and its symmetric square is
    \texorpdfstring{$\JPi$}{J_Π}-even, so the
    primary variable must be an antisymmetric chiral two-form \texorpdfstring{$\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$}{F_{μν}(s)}.
    \texorpdfstring{$\Bsat(s)$}{B_sat(s)} is defined as the Born--Infeld determinantal saturation margin of such a
    supplied
    two-form, with the sign fixed by the admissibility role of axiom A4, and its second variation
    \texorpdfstring{$\muchi = \tfrac12 \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}$}{μ_χ² = ½ P_{μν} P^{μν}} is the Lorentzian genus
    of the
    chiral polarisation \texorpdfstring{$\Pchi$}{P}.
    Premises that no source supplies are stated as hypotheses wherever used: a full-rank Lorentzian co-metric, the
    construction of the two-form from the locking data, the coincidence of the Born--Infeld modulus with the Schur
    tilt, the non-degeneracy of the split at the locked pair, and the hypotheses of the Schur-transverse branch of PRS,
    including the transport of the metaplectic opening to the locking operator.
    The genus is not determined: the central commutator does not supply an electric assignment, since the central
    direction is not temporal in the co-metrics of the corpus and no map from the generation operator to a two-form is
    available.
    For a supplied purely electric two-form the amplitude \texorpdfstring{$|u|$}{|u|} reduces to a transverse cubic
    backreaction that vanishes on the real symplectic cascade of the model, so the amplitude mechanism is Born--Infeld
    saturation; the magnitude \texorpdfstring{$|u|$}{|u|} is not predicted and stays dictionary-bound.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position and purpose}
    \label{sec:position}

    The fermionic sub-programme locates the three-generation mass split in the $\Jthree$-odd part of the squared projective
    endomorphism $\Epi^2$ restricted to the gauge-singlet generation triplet $\Cgen$, parametrised by a single real number
    $u$ through $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen} = \diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, with the even sector
    $\diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, the algebraic value of $(C_2-J_3^2)/C_2$ at $C_2=2$.
    Its reading as the Born--Infeld even sector would require an identification between the
    conditional $3\times3$ model of~\cite{Beau2026a34} on $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$ and
    $(E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}$; no such identification is available, so that reading is not used
    here.
    The Schur reduction~\cite{Beau2026prs} fixed the structural status of $u$: it is controlled by the
    $D_{\pm}$-transported,
    generation-projected component of the antiunitary chiral equivariance defect
    $\Delta_\chi(P) = \pi_{LL} - \tau \pi_{RR} \tau^{-1}$ of the eliminated block $1 - P$, it vanishes for the chirally
    symmetric minimal non-injectivity, and a non-zero value requires a chiral symmetry-breaking carried by the
    projection-locking axiom A4.
    The finite sector was excluded as a source: finite projection locking is $\JPi$-equivariant, so $u_{\mathrm{fin}} = 0$
    for
    $q$ an odd prime, and the remaining obstruction is entirely Lorentzian~\cite{Beau2026prs}.
    The split is then governed by the chiral curvature $\muchi := \partial_s^2 \Bsat(0)$ of a saturation functional $\Bsat$
    along the $\JPi$-odd modulus $s$ opened by Lorentzian complexification, with the trichotomy: $\muchi > 0$ gives a
    $\JPi$-equivariant stable point and $u = 0$; $\muchi < 0$ gives a $\JPi$-conjugate saturated pair $\pm s_*$ and a
    split $u \neq 0$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}; $\muchi = 0$ is marginal.

    This note settles the structural prerequisites for $\muchi$ and states the premises under which its sign is read.
    Section~\ref{sec:oriented} records that the modulus is $\JPi$-odd and oriented, and recalls its discrete avatar.

    Section~\ref{sec:genus} examines the genus of the chiral polarisation: the central commutator does not supply an

    Section~\ref{sec:amplitude} extends the radicand to quartic order and reduces the amplitude to the sign of the cubic

  \section{The chiral modulus is $\JPi$-odd and oriented}
    \label{sec:oriented}

    The spinorial Born--Infeld parity lift is the antiunitary structure $\JPi = \epsilon \circ \mathrm{conj}$ with
    $\epsilon = i\sigma_y$, giving $\JPi^2 = -1$ on the fundamental doublet~\cite{Beau2026q14, Beau2026prs}.
    On the generation triplet $\Cgen = \mathrm{span}(e_0, e_+, e_-)$ the induced involution $\Jtwo$ satisfies
    $(\Jtwo)^2 = +1$, with $\Jtwo e_0 = -e_0$ and $\Jtwo e_\pm = e_\mp$, and consequently
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Jtwo-Jthree}
      \Jtwo \, \Jthree \, (\Jtwo)^{-1} = -\Jthree ,
    \end{equation}
    so that the Born--Infeld conjugation exchanges the two outer generation weights~\cite{Beau2026prs}.

    \begin{lemma}[Oddness of the split]
      \label{lem:u-odd}
      Under $\Jtwo$ the split coefficient transforms as $u \mapsto -u$.
      If $s$ denotes the $\JPi$-odd modulus, with $\JPi : s \mapsto -s$, then $u = u(s)$ is an odd function,
      $u(-s) = -u(s)$, and in particular $u(0) = 0$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The triplet endomorphism $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen} = \diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ is written in the weight basis
      $(e_0, e_+, e_-)$.
      Since $\Jtwo$ swaps $e_+ \leftrightarrow e_-$ by~\eqref{eq:Jtwo-Jthree}, conjugation by $\Jtwo$ sends this endomorphism
      to
      $\diag(1, \tfrac12 - u, \tfrac12 + u)$, i.e.\ $u \mapsto -u$.
      The defect $\Delta_\chi(P)$ and hence $u$ are odd in the modulus $s$ opened by Lorentzian
      complexification~\cite{Beau2026prs}, with $\JPi$ acting as $s \mapsto -s$; oddness of $u(s)$ and $u(0) = 0$ follow.
    \end{proof}

    The orientation carried by the modulus is not an artefact of the chiral frame.
    Its discrete avatar is the oriented frontier observable of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}: on the directed outgoing edges
    $g \to gs$ of the cascade the central increment is the Heisenberg cocycle
    $\Delta A_c(g, s) = c\,(z(s) + a(g) s_b)$, reduced to $c\,a(g) s_b$ on admissible generators, which measures the dynamic
    $\Jthree$ rather than a free shear.
    This increment is linear in the orientation datum $s_b$ and therefore odd under orientation reversal.
    Its symmetric average vanishes identically, $\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0$ on symmetric shells~\cite{Beau2026aar},
    which is exactly the statement that a symmetrisation annihilates an orientation-odd signal.

  \section{Antisymmetric structure of the chiral modulus}
    \label{sec:antisymmetry}

    We now show that the carrier of the $\JPi$-odd modulus must be an antisymmetric two-form, not a symmetric square.
    Let $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$ be a candidate two-form built from the chiral-odd deformation, with the linear expansion
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Fchi-linear}
      \Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s) = s\,\Pchi_{\mu\nu} + O(s^2), \qquad \Pchi := \partial_s \Fchi |_{s=0} ,
    \end{equation}
    and let
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Mdef}
      M^{\mu}{}_{\nu}(s) := \Fchi^{\mu\rho}(s)\, \Fchi_{\rho\nu}(s)
    \end{equation}
    be the symmetric positive combination obtained by squaring it.
    The antiunitary lift acts on the two-form by $\JPi : \Fchi(s) \mapsto \Fchi(-s)$, that is by $\Fchi \mapsto -\Fchi$ at
    linear order in $s$, consistently with Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}.

    \begin{lemma}[Antisymmetric carrier]
      \label{lem:antisymmetry}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      The symmetric square $M(s)$ is $\JPi$-even and stationary at the symmetric point:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:M-even}
        M(-s) = M(s), \qquad M(0) = 0, \qquad \partial_s M(0) = 0 .
      \end{equation}
      Consequently every scalar functional $\Phi[M]$ is even in $s$ to the order at which $M$ is even and obeys
      $\partial_s \Phi[M]|_{s=0} = 0$; it assigns the same value to the two $\JPi$-conjugate branches $\pm s_*$ and is
      therefore
      blind to the sign of $u$.
      The split modulus cannot be carried by $M$.
      A primary variable that resolves the two branches must change sign under $s \mapsto -s$, hence must be the antisymmetric
      two-form $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, $\Fchi(-s) = -\Fchi(s)$, so by~\eqref{eq:Mdef}
      $M = \Fchi \Fchi \mapsto (-\Fchi)(-\Fchi) = M$: the square is $\JPi$-invariant.
      Substituting~\eqref{eq:Fchi-linear} gives $M(s) = s^2\, \Pchi^{\mu\rho} \Pchi_{\rho\nu} + O(s^3)$, whence
      $M(0) = 0$ and $\partial_s M(0) = 0$, which is~\eqref{eq:M-even}.
      For any functional $\Phi[M]$ the chain rule gives
      $\partial_s \Phi[M]|_{0} = (\delta\Phi/\delta M) : \partial_s M|_{0} = 0$, so $\Phi$ has no linear response to the
      modulus.
      The branch choice is the sign of $u(s_*) = -u(-s_*)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}; distinguishing $+s_*$ from
      $-s_*$ requires a quantity odd in $s$, which $\Phi[M]$ is not.
      Therefore $M$ cannot be the primary modulus variable.
      A two-form $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$ satisfying $\Fchi(-s) = -\Fchi(s)$ at linear order does change sign and reproduces the
      orientation-odd increment of the discrete frontier observable~\cite{Beau2026q11of, Beau2026aar}; it is the minimal
      carrier
      compatible with both the $\JPi$-oddness of $u$ and the oriented cascade datum.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Role of the symmetric square]
      \label{cor:M-backreaction}
      The symmetric combination $M = \Fchi \Fchi$ enters the saturation functional only at second order in $s$, as a
      $\JPi$-even saturating backreaction or higher-order term.
      It is excluded as the primary modulus variable but admissible as a subleading contribution.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By~\eqref{eq:M-even} the leading occurrence of $M$ is $O(s^2)$ and $\JPi$-even, which is the order and parity of a
      backreaction term rather than of the linear modulus carrier of Lemma~\ref{lem:antisymmetry}.
    \end{proof}

    The lemma resolves the antisymmetric/symmetric fork implicit in the definition deliverable of~\cite{Beau2026prs}:
    the chiral modulus is read off an
    antisymmetric two-form, and the non-abelian symmetric replacement $M^{\mu}{}_{\nu} = \Fchi^{\mu\rho} \Fchi_{\rho\nu}$
    used
    elsewhere to remove a Pfaffian sign ambiguity is precisely what must be avoided at the level of the primary variable,
    because removing the sign ambiguity removes the orientation that carries the split.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Construction of the chiral two-form, {[H-F]}]
      \label{hyp:chiral-two-form}
      There is an injective linear map $\iota$ from the $\JPi$-odd part of $\mathrm{End}(\Cgen)$ on the outer weights
      into real two-forms of the effective spacetime, and the chiral two-form is
      $\Fchi(s)=\iota\big(\Pi_S D\,(P(s)-P(0))\,D\,\Pi_S^*\big|_{\mathrm{gen},\,\JPi\text{-odd}}\big)$, exactly odd,
      $\Fchi(-s)=-\Fchi(s)$, with $\Fchi(s)=s\,\Pchi+O(s^3)$.
      Here $s$ is the Schur modulus of the locking operator.
      No source supplies $\iota$: the generation operator $\Jthree$ acts on the generation factor, a Clifford bivector
      acts on the spinor factor, and the two carriers are distinct.
      It is a missing element, not an excluded one.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Non-degeneracy of the split at the saturated pair, {[H-Nd]}]
      \label{hyp:nondegenerate}
      When $\muchi<0$, the radicand $\Dchi$ reaches its bound at a finite contact $s_*>0$, which gives a
      $\JPi$-conjugate saturated pair $\pm s_*$ of the locking, and the odd carrier $u(s)$ of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd} does not vanish at that pair.
      Oddness gives $u(0)=0$ and $u(-s)=-u(s)$ only.
    \end{hypothesis}

  \section{The Born--Infeld saturation margin}
    \label{sec:margin}

    We now define the functional whose chiral curvature governs the split.
    Let $g_{\mu\nu}$ be an effective metric of Lorentzian signature and let $\beta$ be the
    universal Born--Infeld saturation scale of the bounded-flux completion~\cite{Beau2026c}.
    Born--Infeld is the unique local completion compatible with a finite transport capacity, and its determinantal action is
    carried by an antisymmetric field strength~\cite{Beau2026c}; this is the structure into which the antisymmetric two-form
    $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$ of Section~\ref{sec:antisymmetry} enters.

    \begin{definition}[Chiral admissibility radicand and saturation margin]
      \label{def:B}
      Let
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:Dchi}
        \Dchi(s) := \frac{-\det\!\big(g_{\mu\nu} + \beta^{-1} \Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)\big)}{-\det g_{\mu\nu}} ,
      \end{equation}
      the dimensionless ratio in which the metric determinant cancels.
      Admissibility of the chiral-odd deformation is the reality and non-negativity of the radicand, $\Dchi(s) \geq 0$, and
      saturation is its approach to the bound $\Dchi(s) \to 0$.
      The Born--Infeld saturation margin along the modulus is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:B}
        \Bsat(s) := \beta^2 \left( \sqrt{\Dchi(s)} - 1 \right) .
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    The functional $\Bsat$ is fixed in its role, not merely in its formula.
    By the projection-locking theorem~\cite{Beau2026a26} and axiom A4~\cite{Beau2026m}, resolution occurs only where the
    continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the discrete shell support; the physically locked chiral configuration is the
    saturated admissible extremum selected on that support.
    We adopt the variational reading in which $\Bsat$ is minimised at locking, so that the symmetric point $s = 0$ is the
    selected configuration exactly when it is a stable minimum of $\Bsat$.

    \begin{remark}[The convention sign-trap]
      \label{rem:trap}
      The determinantal scalar~\eqref{eq:B} admits an overall sign ambiguity according to the object it names.
      Read as the Born--Infeld action density, it is extremised by the equations of motion and recovers a Maxwell-type
      weak-field expansion; read as the saturation margin, it is minimised at the locking event of A4.
      The two readings differ by an overall sign, and that sign propagates directly into $\partial_s^2 \Bsat(0)$.
      Choosing the sign so as to obtain a desired trichotomy would make the conclusion an artefact of convention rather than a
      derived result.
      We therefore fix the sign once, by the admissibility role of $\Bsat$ (Definition~\ref{def:B}, anchored
      in~\cite{Beau2026a26, Beau2026m}), independently of and prior to any knowledge of the sign of the second variation.
      With this fixing, the sign of the split is never inferred from the convention: it is carried solely by the Lorentzian
      genus of $\Pchi$ computed in Section~\ref{sec:reduction}.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{proposition}[Parity of the margin]
      \label{prop:parity}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      $\Bsat(s) = \Bsat(-s)$, so $s = 0$ is automatically a stationary point of $\Bsat$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the two-form obeys $\Fchi(-s) = -\Fchi(s)$, and
      the determinant
      in~\eqref{eq:Dchi}
      depends on $\Fchi$ only through even powers, since for an antisymmetric argument the odd elementary symmetric functions
      of
      $g^{-1}\Fchi$ vanish.
      Hence $\Dchi(s) = \Dchi(-s)$ and $\Bsat(s) = \Bsat(-s)$ by~\eqref{eq:B}.
      Evenness gives $\partial_s \Bsat(0) = 0$.
      This is the saturation-functional avatar of Born--Infeld parity, which acts covariantly
      on the BI response family, preserving even-order responses
      (O18 Proposition~2.6)~\cite{Beau2026a22}.
      The value $\diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ is algebraic; attributing it a Born--Infeld origin
      would require the identification declared unavailable above, and is not used here.
    \end{proof}

  \section{Second variation and the Lorentzian reduction}
    \label{sec:reduction}

    We expand the radicand to the order needed for the chiral curvature.
    The standard four-dimensional Born--Infeld determinant identity for an antisymmetric two-form gives
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:det-expansion}
      \Dchi(s) = 1 + \frac{1}{2\beta^2}\, \Fchi_{\mu\nu} \Fchi^{\mu\nu}
      - \frac{1}{16\beta^4}\, \big(\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Fchi}^{\mu\nu}\big)^2 ,
    \end{equation}
    where indices are raised with the effective co-metric and $\widetilde{\Fchi}$ is the Hodge dual.
    Substituting the linear expansion~\eqref{eq:Fchi-linear}, the pseudoscalar term is $O(s^4)$ and does not contribute to
    the
    second variation, so
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Dchi-quadratic}
      \Dchi(s) = 1 + \frac{s^2}{2\beta^2}\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} + O(s^4) .
    \end{equation}

    \begin{theorem}[Lorentzian reduction of the chiral curvature]
      \label{thm:reduction}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian}, \ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} and~\ref{lem:coincidence}.
      With $\Bsat$ as in Definition~\ref{def:B},
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:muchi}
        \muchi := \partial_s^2 \Bsat(0) = \tfrac12\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} ,
      \end{equation}
      so that $\operatorname{sign}(\muchi) = \operatorname{sign}\!\big(\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}\big)$, the Lorentzian
      genus
      of the chiral polarisation $\Pchi$ in the effective co-metric.
      A spacelike (magnetic-type) $\Pchi$ gives $\muchi > 0$, a stable symmetric point, and $u = 0$; a timelike
      (electric-type) $\Pchi$ gives $\muchi < 0$, an unstable symmetric point, and, under
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}, a $\JPi$-conjugate saturated
      pair $\pm s_*$ carrying a split $u \neq 0$ whose sign labels the two branches; a null
      $\Pchi$ gives $\muchi = 0$, the marginal case.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      From~\eqref{eq:B} and~\eqref{eq:Dchi-quadratic},
      $\Bsat(s) = \beta^2 \big( \sqrt{1 + \tfrac{s^2}{2\beta^2} \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}} - 1 \big)
      = \tfrac{s^2}{4}\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} + O(s^4)$, giving~\eqref{eq:muchi}.
      In an orthonormal coframe of the Lorentzian co-metric the contraction $\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}$ is the
      Minkowski contraction, which for an antisymmetric two-form is
      $2(|\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}|^2 - |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2)$,
      positive for a magnetic-type (spacelike-dominated) polarisation and negative for an electric-type
      (timelike-dominated) one.
      The mapping to the trichotomy then follows from the variational reading of Definition~\ref{def:B}: at $\muchi > 0$ the
      symmetric point is a stable minimum and is selected ($u = 0$); at $\muchi < 0$ it is a local maximum, and the
      deformation
      selects, by the variational reading, the saturated admissible pair $\pm s_*$; its existence and the non-vanishing
      of $u(\pm s_*)=\mp u(s_*)$ are the content of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate} (Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd} gives only
      the oddness), the overall sign labelling the two branches; at $\muchi = 0$ the second
      variation is degenerate and the locking
      character is decided by the leading non-vanishing even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$ of
      $\Bsat(s) = \Bsat(0) + \lambda_{2k} s^{2k} + O(s^{2k+2})$.
    \end{proof}

    The reduction has a transparent Born--Infeld meaning.
    A timelike chiral polarisation drives the radicand $\Dchi$ toward zero, so the chiral-odd deformation reaches saturation
    at
    finite $|s|$ and A4 can lock a non-trivial pair; a spacelike polarisation increases the radicand, never saturates, and
    leaves the symmetric point as the only locked configuration.
    This is the chiral instance of the canonical Born--Infeld feature that electric-type invariants saturate the bound while
    magnetic-type invariants do not.

  \section{The coincidence lock}
    \label{sec:coincidence}

    The reduction of Section~\ref{sec:reduction} presupposes that the modulus entering the saturation
    margin~\eqref{eq:B} is tied to the $\JPi$-odd tilt of the eliminated Schur block.
    A priori these are two distinct quantities: the projector tilt $s_{\mathrm{proj}}$, defined at the level of the
    projection algebra through the deformation $1 - P(s)$ of the admissible projector $P = \Pi_S^* \Pi_S$, and the
    saturation
    modulus $s_{\mathrm{BI}}$, the chiral-odd companion of the Higgs amplitude mode of the saturated condensate.
    We motivate the lock by transposing the projection-locking theorem to the $\JPi$-odd sector.

    The axiom A4 is an intersection scheme, not a saturation criterion~\cite{Beau2026m, Beau2026a26}:
    resolution occurs where the continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the available discrete support of the projection,
    and the transition becomes structurally discrete.
    Transposed to the $\JPi$-odd sector, a continuous chiral-odd modulus is observable only if it is encoded by the
    projection.
    Since the observable space is $\mathcal{O} = \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$ and the Schur complement
    $\Epi = -\Pi_S D (1 - P) D \Pi_S^*$ sources the chiral structure through the eliminated block $1 - P$, a chiral-odd
    Born--Infeld deformation is realisable only as a tilt of the complexified block $1 - P^{\mathbb{C}}$, and conversely a
    tilt of $1 - P^{\mathbb{C}}$ is dynamically present only on the saturation locus.
    On this reading the two moduli are not independent on the locus.
    At the finite level this tilt vanishes, $\partial_s ( 1 - P_{\mathrm{fin}}(s) )|_{0} = 0$ by the $\JPi$-equivariance of
    finite locking~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so the modulus is carried entirely by the Lorentzian completion.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Tangential coincidence on the A4 locus, {[H-Coin]}]
      \label{lem:coincidence}
      On the chiral-odd A4 locking locus the Born--Infeld modulus and the Schur tilt are related by a non-degenerate
      reparametrisation,
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:coincidence}
        s_{\mathrm{BI}} = \phi(s_{\mathrm{proj}}), \qquad \phi(0) = 0, \qquad \phi'(0) \neq 0 ,
      \end{equation}
      so that A4 identifies their first-order transverse direction up to non-degenerate reparametrisation.
      This is a tangential identification of the $\JPi$-odd direction, not a strict identification of the parameters.
      It is not derived from the Schur transversality or from the projection-locking theorem.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{corollary}[Reparametrisation-invariance of the genus]
      \label{cor:reparam}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence}.
      The sign of the chiral curvature is invariant under the locking reparametrisation:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:reparam}
        \partial_{s_{\mathrm{proj}}}^2 \Bsat(0) = \phi'(0)^2 \, \partial_{s_{\mathrm{BI}}}^2 \Bsat(0) ,
      \end{equation}
      so $\operatorname{sign}(\muchi)$ is independent of which modulus parametrises $\Bsat$, and the genus reduction of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is well-posed.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Equation~\eqref{eq:reparam} is the chain rule for $\Bsat \circ \phi$ at $\phi(0) = 0$, using $\partial_s \Bsat(0) = 0$
      from Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}; the leading term is $\phi'(0)^2 \, \partial_{s_{\mathrm{BI}}}^2 \Bsat(0)$, and the
      positive factor $\phi'(0)^2$ leaves the sign unchanged.
    \end{proof}

    The first-order projector variation supplies the chiral polarisation explicitly.
    Differentiating $\Epi(s) = -\Pi_S D (1 - P(s)) D \Pi_S^*$ at $s = 0$ with $D$ and $\Pi_S$ held fixed, and using
    $\partial_s [-(1 - P(s))] = \partial_s P(s)$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Pchi-schur}
      \partial_s \Epi|_{0} = \Pi_S D (\partial_s P|_{0}) D \Pi_S^* ,
      \qquad
      \Pchi = \iota\Big(\Pi_S D (\partial_s P|_{0}) D \Pi_S^* \big|_{\mathrm{gen},\,\JPi\text{-odd}}\Big)
      \quad\text{(Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form})} .
    \end{equation}
    The positive sign is the derivative of $-(1 - P)$, not a hidden conventional choice; it is fixed by the Schur form, in
    keeping with the convention discipline of Section~\ref{sec:margin}.

    \begin{definition}[Schur transversality on the $\JPi$-odd eliminated sector]
      \label{def:transversality}
      The locking is Schur-transverse if the direction opened by $\partial_s P|_{0}$ does not lie in the kernel of the Schur
      transport, that is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:transversality}
        \Pi_S D (\partial_s P|_{0}) D \Pi_S^* \big|_{\mathrm{gen},\,\JPi\text{-odd}} \neq 0 ,
      \end{equation}
      equivalently the non-vanishing of $X \mapsto \Pi_S D X D \Pi_S^* |_{\mathrm{gen},\,\JPi\text{-odd}}$ on the direction
      $\partial_s P|_{0}$.
      Injectivity of this transport on the whole $\JPi$-odd eliminated subspace is sufficient but stronger than required; the
      argument depends only on transversality of the opened direction.
      This is the notion of PRS, where it is formulated for the $\JPi$-odd part $\sigma$ of $\partial_s P|_0$ as
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\neq0$; the two agree under the $\JPi$-compatibility stated there.
    \end{definition}

    The discharge is then a dichotomy.

    \begin{proposition}[Discharge dichotomy (after the Schur reduction)]
      \label{prop:dichotomy}
      The transverse/null dichotomy is established at operator level in the Schur reduction~\cite{Beau2026prs}
      under the hypotheses stated there; transposed to the A4 locus through Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}
      ($\iota$ linear and injective) and~\ref{lem:coincidence} it reads as follows.
      On the chiral-odd A4 locus:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item if the locking is Schur-transverse and Hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence} holds, the Born--Infeld modulus
        and the Schur tilt define the same $\JPi$-odd tangent direction with $\phi'(0) \neq 0$, and the reduction
        $\muchi = \tfrac12 \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is the genus computation;
        \item if the locking is Schur-null, then $\Pchi = 0$ and $\muchi = 0$ at quadratic order, the marginal branch
        of the trichotomy of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}, decided by the first non-vanishing even coefficient
        $\lambda_{2k}$ rather than directly by $u = 0$.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{proposition}

    The coincidence lock is the hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence}.
    PRS~\cite{Beau2026prs} proves the Schur-null / Schur-transverse dichotomy under explicit hypotheses and leaves
    the branch open: the oriented metaplectic step has the exact $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ opening
    $\alpha(t,s)=ts\,r/\sinh r\not\equiv0$ with $\mu(t,s)\equiv0$, and its transport to $\partial_s P|_{0}$ is the
    transport hypothesis [H-Tr] of PRS.
    Under Hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence}, Corollary~\ref{cor:reparam} shows that the genus does not depend on the
    positive Jacobian $\phi'(0)$.

  \section{The genus of the chiral polarisation}
    \label{sec:genus}

    The genus of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is the sign of $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}$, fixed by the index structure
    of the chiral polarisation~\eqref{eq:Pchi-schur} once a two-form carrier is supplied
    (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}).
    The projected Dirac square has the Lichnerowicz form
    $E=\tfrac{R}{4}+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu}T^a+\Epi$~\cite{Beau2026q14, Beau2026prs}; its bivector
    term is the gauge curvature, a summand distinct from $\Epi$, so no source writes the chiral polarisation as a
    Clifford bivector.
    For a supplied two-form the electric and magnetic parts are $\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P, i} = \Pchi_{0i}$ and
    $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P, i} = \tfrac12 \epsilon_{ijk} \Pchi_{jk}$ in an orthonormal coframe, the decomposition used
    in Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}.

    \begin{lemma}[Genus of a supplied chiral two-form]
      \label{lem:index-assignment}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      In an orthonormal coframe of the effective co-metric,
      $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}=2\big(|\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}|^2-|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2\big)$ and
      $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu}\propto\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}$.
      Hence a purely electric two-form, $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}=0\neq\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}$, has
      $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}=-2|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2<0$, $\muchi<0$ and vanishing pseudoscalar, and a
      purely
      magnetic one has $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}>0$ and $\muchi>0$.
      The data of this note do not determine which of $\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}$, $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}$ is non-zero.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      These are the standard decompositions of an antisymmetric two-form in an orthonormal Lorentzian coframe.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The central bracket does not supply an electric assignment]
      \label{rem:bracket}
      The Lie bracket $[X, Y] = Z$ is the algebraic generation of the central direction from the horizontal plane, not
      its metric index.
      An electric assignment of the $\Jthree$-odd signal would pair the central direction with $\gamma^0$, that is
      identify $\partial_\tau$ with $Z$.
      In the co-metric of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} (Theorem~5.2) the central entry is zero; in the conditional
      completion of Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} (Corollary~6.1) it is positive; in Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} $\tau$ is a separate
      factor with a free coefficient.
      The central direction is temporal in none of them.
      Identifying $\partial_\tau$ with $Z$ would abandon the product structure of Q5b (Remark~3.3) and is a new
      geometric proposal, not an inherited identification; keeping $\tau$ temporal while making $Z$ temporal would give
      two temporal directions.
      The coefficient $\alpha$ of the $\Jthree$-signal is produced by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator $[E,F]=H$ of
      the two shear generators (Q14, Proposition~6.5), not by the Heisenberg bracket.
      Finally, no map from $\Jthree\in\mathrm{End}(\Cgen)$ to the components of a two-form is supplied
      (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}).
      Neither an electric nor a magnetic genus therefore follows from the central bracket.
    \end{remark}

    The genus deferred in Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is not determined here.
    For a supplied purely electric two-form, the amplitude analysis of Section~\ref{sec:amplitude} applies, and what
    remains is the amplitude $|u|$, the A4 matching of the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation with $\muchi$, which
    is not a genus question.

  \section{The amplitude frontier}
    \label{sec:amplitude}

    The genus fixes the sign of $\muchi$ and hence the existence of the split; it does not fix the magnitude $|u|$.
    To locate the saturated pair $\pm s_*$ we extend the radicand beyond the second variation.
    Because $\Bsat$ is a monotone function of $\Dchi$ through~\eqref{eq:B}, the interior stationary points of $\Bsat$
    and of $\Dchi$ coincide, and the square-root concavity changes only the depth of the locked configuration, not its
    position.
    The operative object is therefore the quartic of $\Dchi$, not that of $\Bsat$.

    By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the two-form is an odd function of the modulus, refining the linear
    expansion~\eqref{eq:Fchi-linear} to
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Fchi-cubic}
      \Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s) = s\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} + s^3\, \mathcal{R}_{\chi,\mu\nu} + O(s^5), \qquad
      \mathcal{R}_{\chi,\mu\nu} := \tfrac{1}{3!}\, \partial_s^3 \Fchi_{\mu\nu}\big|_{s=0} ,
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is the cubic response of the chiral two-form, the leading bending of the modulus ray away
    from its linear direction $\Pchi$.
    We write $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} := \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\,\mu\nu}$ for the contraction.

    \begin{proposition}[Quartic of the chiral radicand]
      \label{prop:quartic}
      With the determinantal identity~\eqref{eq:det-expansion} and the odd expansion~\eqref{eq:Fchi-cubic},
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:Dchi-quartic}
        \Dchi(s) = 1 + \frac{1}{2\beta^2}\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}\, s^2
        + \left( \frac{\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}}{\beta^2}
            - \frac{\big(\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu}\big)^2}{16\beta^4} \right) s^4 + O(s^6) .
      \end{equation}
      The quartic carries two contributions of distinct status: a Born--Infeld pseudoscalar term, manifestly
      non-positive and fixed by $\Pchi$ alone, and a backreaction term $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ whose sign
      is not fixed by $\Pchi$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The exact four-dimensional identity~\eqref{eq:det-expansion} is quadratic in the two invariants
      $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \Fchi^{\mu\nu}$ and $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Fchi}^{\mu\nu}$.
      Substituting~\eqref{eq:Fchi-cubic}, the scalar invariant is
      $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \Fchi^{\mu\nu} = s^2\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} + 2 s^4\, \Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}
      + O(s^6)$, and the pseudoscalar invariant is
      $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Fchi}^{\mu\nu} = s^2\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu} + O(s^4)$,
      so its square is $O(s^4)$.
      Collecting the $s^4$ coefficients gives~\eqref{eq:Dchi-quartic}.
      The pseudoscalar term $-\big(\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu}\big)^2 / 16\beta^4 \leq 0$ depends only on
      $\Pchi$; the backreaction term is linear in $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$, an independent datum of the chiral two-form.
    \end{proof}

    Born--Infeld therefore supplies no quartic stabiliser of its own: the square root is concave and the pseudoscalar
    contribution is non-positive.
    Any interior stabilisation must come from the backreaction $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$, and in its absence the modulus runs
    toward the saturation bound $\Dchi \to 0$.

    For a supplied purely electric two-form (Lemma~\ref{lem:index-assignment}) the pseudoscalar term vanishes:
    $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P} = 0$, the pseudoscalar invariant is
    $\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu} \propto \mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P} \cdot \mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P} = 0$, so
    the destabilising contribution of Proposition~\ref{prop:quartic} is absent.
    Writing $\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} = 2(|\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}|^2 - |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2)
    = -2|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2$ for such a two-form, the radicand reduces to
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Dchi-electric}
      \Dchi(s) = 1 - \frac{|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2}{\beta^2}\, s^2
      + \frac{\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}}{\beta^2}\, s^4 + O(s^6) ,
    \end{equation}
    so the quartic of the chiral radicand is carried entirely by the backreaction $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$.

    \begin{proposition}[Coordinate-level boundary-versus-interior selection]
      \label{prop:amplitude-dichotomy}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      Fix an odd local modulus $s$ for the chiral two-form trajectory.
      For a supplied purely electric two-form the quartic radicand in that parametrisation is governed by the
      coefficient
      $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ through the following coordinate-level dichotomy.
      If $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} > 0$ the radicand~\eqref{eq:Dchi-electric} has an interior minimum candidate at
      \[
        s_*^2 = \frac{|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2}{2\, \Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}} ,
      \]
      which defines a physical interior lock only if this minimum survives modulo odd reparametrisation and lies
      before the first saturation contact, $\Dchi(s_*) > 0$.
      If $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} \leq 0$ no quartic minimum is created in this parametrisation, and the
      amplitude is fixed by the first contact with the saturation bound, $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Stationarity of~\eqref{eq:Dchi-electric} at $s > 0$ requires
      $-2 |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2 \beta^{-2} s + 4\, (\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}) \beta^{-2} s^3 = 0$, with the
      non-trivial root $s_*^2 = |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2 / (2\, \Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi})$, real and positive
      exactly when $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} > 0$.
      At this root one has
      \[
        \partial_s^2 \Dchi(s_*) = -\frac{2|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2}{\beta^2}
        + \frac{12\,(\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi})}{\beta^2}\, s_*^2
        = \frac{4|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2}{\beta^2} > 0 ,
      \]
      so the root is an interior minimum of the quartic radicand.
      For $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} \leq 0$, both the quadratic and quartic coefficients
      in~\eqref{eq:Dchi-electric} are non-positive, so no positive interior minimum is created at this order.
      The admissible region is then terminated by the first contact with the saturation locus $\Dchi(s) = 0$,
      where $\Bsat$ reaches its lower bound $-\beta^2$ and A4 locks.
    \end{proof}

    This proposition is a statement about the quartic expansion in a fixed odd modulus.
    Its invariant content is supplied only after quotienting the $\Pchi$-parallel cubic response by odd
    reparametrisation, as recorded in the Open paragraph of Section~\ref{sec:status}.

    \begin{corollary}[Radial contact in the affine electric gauge]
      \label{cor:radial-contact}
      For a supplied purely electric two-form in the Schur-transverse branch of PRS (under its hypotheses), under
      Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}
      and~\ref{lem:coincidence}, and after quotienting the $\Pchi$-parallel cubic response by odd
      reparametrisation, the affine radial contact of the Born--Infeld radicand is fixed by $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$.
      Writing $a := |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2 / \beta^2$ and $b := \Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} / \beta^2$, the
      boundary branch of $\Dchi(s) = 1 - a s^2 + b s^4 + O(s^6)$ has, when $4b\le a^2$, the positive contact
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:s-star-contact}
        s_*^2 = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - 4b/a^2}}{2b/a}
        = \frac{1}{a}\left( 1 + \frac{b}{a^2} + O(b^2/a^4) \right) ,
      \end{equation}
      whenever the quartic truncation is used away from an interior lock.
      Equivalently,
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:s-star-value}
        s_* = \frac{\beta}{|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|}
        \left( 1 + \frac{\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}\, \beta^2}{2 |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^4}
        + O\!\left( \frac{(\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi})^2 \beta^4}{|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^8} \right) \right) .
      \end{equation}
      In the affine electric gauge in which the transverse cubic backreaction vanishes, this reduces to the radial
      contact
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:s-star-affine}
        s_* = \frac{\beta}{|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|} .
      \end{equation}
      This fixes the radial contact $s_*$ entering the contact split.
      It does not fix the absolute magnitude of the generation split, because $\beta$, $|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|$, and
      the chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ remain upstream normalisation data.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      The contact condition in the quartic truncation is $1 - a s^2 + b s^4 = 0$.
      With $x = s^2$, the boundary root is the branch continuously connected to $x = 1/a$ as $b \to 0$,
      \[
        x = \frac{a - \sqrt{a^2 - 4b}}{2b} = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - 4b/a^2}}{2b/a} .
      \]
      Expanding the square root gives $x = a^{-1}\,( 1 + b/a^2 + O(b^2/a^4) )$, and taking the positive square root gives
      the stated expression for $s_*$.
      When the transverse cubic response vanishes in the affine electric gauge, $b = 0$ in the physical quotient and the
      first contact is exactly $s_* = \beta/|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Not a Landau closure]
      \label{rem:not-landau}
      The interior branch $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} > 0$ has the local form of a quartic double well, but the
      stabilisation is not a Landau mechanism: it is conditional on the interior minimum candidate preceding the
      Born--Infeld saturation wall.
      The natural locking mechanism remains saturation at $\Dchi = 0$, recovered whenever the backreaction fails to turn
      the radicand before that contact.
      The amplitude is thus a bounded-flux selection problem, not a polynomial minimisation, and the magnitude $|u|$
      follows from $s_*$ only after the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ maps the modulus to the
      split through the odd carrier $u(s)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}.
    \end{remark}

    The cubic response is itself a transported Schur datum.
    Since the Schur form $\Epi = -\Pi_S D (1 - P) D \Pi_S^*$ is linear in $P$ at fixed $D, \Pi_S$~\cite{Beau2026prs},
    the transport acts term by term on the modulus expansion, so, under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form},
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Rchi-transport}
      \mathcal{R}_{\chi} = \tfrac{1}{3!}\, \iota\Big(\Pi_S D \big(\partial_s^3 P|_{0}\big) D \Pi_S^*
      \big|_{\mathrm{gen},\,\JPi\text{-odd}}\Big) ,
    \end{equation}
    the same transport as the polarisation~\eqref{eq:Pchi-schur} applied to the third-order tilt.
    The projector constraint $P^2 = P$, which holds when $\Pi_S$ is a partial isometry~\cite{Beau2026prs}, fixes only
    the $P_0$-diagonal part of each derivative in terms of lower ones;
    the off-diagonal cubic part that sources~\eqref{eq:Rchi-transport} is unconstrained.
    Hence the sign of $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is not fixed by $P^2 = P$, nor by the Lorentzian
    complexification, which supplies the admissible directions but not the curvature of the projector path; it is a
    genuine open datum.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Fixed-generator Lorentzian projector orbit]
      \label{hyp:fixed-generator-orbit}
      The Lorentzian complexification of the eliminated spinorial block is realised, to the order relevant for the
      chiral amplitude, by a fixed generator $X$,
      \[
        P(s) = e^{sX} P_0 e^{-sX} .
      \]
      Equivalently, the projector path carries no torsion term in the opened $\JPi$-odd direction up to cubic order.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{remark}[Conditional character of the backreaction]
      \label{rem:conditional-sign}
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:fixed-generator-orbit} is not derived from the present corpus: the complexification fixes
      the tangent direction $X$, not the orbit.
      Under it, $\partial_s^3 P|_{0} = \operatorname{ad}_X^2(\partial_s P|_{0})$, and on the rank-one $\JPi$-odd
      outer-weight direction (the antisymmetric mode $e_+ - e_-$) this collapses to
      \[
        \partial_s^3 P|_{0} = -4|b|^2\, \partial_s P|_{0}, \qquad \mathcal{R}_{\chi} = -\tfrac{2}{3}|b|^2\, \Pchi ,
      \]
      with $b$ the amplitude of $X$ on that mode: the cubic response is parallel to the polarisation.
      A $\Pchi$-parallel $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is pure reparametrisation gauge, removable by the odd modulus
      transformation $\mathcal{R}_{\chi} \mapsto \mathcal{R}_{\chi} + \alpha \Pchi$: the two-form trajectory is then the
      straight ray $\Fchi(s) = (s - \tfrac{2}{3}|b|^2 s^3)\, \Pchi$, the quartic of $\Dchi$ is gaugeable to the marginal
      value, and the apparent interior minimum is only the fold of the non-affine modulus.
      A straight ray has no curvature, so $\Dchi$ decreases monotonically to the bound and the locked amplitude is the
      first saturation contact $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$, the boundary branch of Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy}, in
      agreement with Remark~\ref{rem:not-landau}.
      A genuine interior lock would require a $\Pchi$-transverse component of $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$, absent on the
      rank-one mode and not supplied by a fixed-generator orbit; it is part of the open path-curvature datum.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Path-invariant saturation and the spin obstruction to calibration]
      \label{rem:path-invariant}
      Under the adjoint action of the generation $\mathrm{su}(2)$ the endomorphism algebra decomposes into irreducibles,
      $\mathrm{End}(\Cgen) = \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{5}$, with $\Jthree$ pure spin one and the
      weight-two mixing direction that the transverse cubic response $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ must reach pure spin two, in
      distinct irreducible components.
      By Schur's lemma an $\mathrm{su}(2)$-equivariant transport acts on each component by an independent scalar, so
      calibrating the spin-one (genus) channel of the transport to the supplied polarisation does not fix the spin-two
      (transverse) scalar: the sign of $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is not reachable by genus calibration, confirming
      its open status.
      The transverse cubic response is moreover sourced only by the complex metaplectic phase $\gamma$ of the Lorentzian
      complexification, through the unique weight-balanced bracket combination; the real symplectic cascade of the model
      of~\cite{Beau2026q14} carries $\gamma = 0$ and its $\Jthree$ enters only at second Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff
      order,
      so its transverse cubic response vanishes.
      This vanishing is path-invariant: for a weight-preserving reference $P_0$ the phase generator commutes with $P_0$,
      so a phase adjacent to $P_0$ is a no-op and every ordering of the real cascade gives the same vanishing transverse
      component, for every reference $P_0 = \diag(p_0, p_1, p_2)$.
      Hence the saturation branch does not rest on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:fixed-generator-orbit}: on the real
      cascade of the model the transverse channel is empty independently of the single-generator model, and the
      interior lock requires
      both a complex phase $\gamma \neq 0$, absent from the real cascade of the model, and a placement of that phase in
      the cascade,
      since the transverse cubic datum vanishes when the phase follows the real cascade and is non-zero otherwise, with no
      placement prescribed.
      These statements concern the cascade generator; their transfer to the cubic tilt of the locking operator uses
      the transport hypothesis [H-Tr] of~\cite{Beau2026prs} and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, and the
      Schur's-lemma argument above further assumes that the transport and $\iota$ are $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant,
      which no source supplies.
    \end{remark}

    On the real cascade of the model the boundary-versus-interior selection is therefore resolved in favour of
    saturation (Remark~\ref{rem:path-invariant}); what remains is the doubly conditional interior lock and the magnitude
    $|u|$, fixed by the chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$, whose dictionary status is recorded in
    Section~\ref{sec:status}.

  \section{Boundary contact as the admissible A4 lock}
    \label{sec:boundary}

    The companion eliminated-block note~\cite{Beau2026ebj} proves, in the chiral frame with
    $\JPi = \Sigma_\tau \circ \mathrm{conj}$, $\Sigma_\tau = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & \tau \\ \tau^{-1} & 0
    \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\tau : \mathcal{S}_R \to \mathcal{S}_L$ the unitary intertwiner of~\cite{Beau2026prs}
    with $\tau\,\overline{\tau}^{-1} = \pm 1$, that the left-handed block of the $\JPi$-odd tangent
    of the eliminated block is half the transported equivariance defect rate $\partial_s \Dchi(P)|_0$, with
    $\Dchi(P) = \pi_{LL} - \tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$; this determines one block of the split-sourcing
    tangent, not a unique carrier, the selection of the family and the localisation of the odd tangent onto that block
    remaining open there.
    Throughout this section $\Dchi(P)$ is this equivariance defect, its first-order coefficient being the defect rate,
    while $\Dchi(s)$ is the
    admissibility radicand of Definition~\ref{def:B}: the two share the chiral subscript but are distinct objects,
    distinguished by their argument.
    Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy} stated the boundary-versus-interior dichotomy at coordinate level and
    deferred its invariant content; we supply that content here and select the boundary by admissibility.

    \begin{proposition}[The defect rate is reparametrisation-covariant]
      \label{prop:rate-covariant}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence}, which ties the modulus of $u$ to that of $\Dchi(P)$.
      Under an odd reparametrisation of the modulus $s \mapsto \phi(s)$, $\phi(0) = 0$, $\phi'(0) \neq 0$, the defect
      rate is covariant, not invariant: it rescales by the Jacobian $\phi'(0)$.
      Hence the rate alone carries no chart-independent magnitude; the invariant datum is the split $u(s_*)$ evaluated at a
      geometrically-defined modulus point $s_*$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      $\Dchi(P)$ is a function on the modulus line; the chain rule at $s = 0$, with $\phi(0) = 0$ and
      $\Dchi(P(0)) = 0$ by the finite-locking equivariance~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so that the first-order coefficient is
      the leading term, makes that coefficient scale by $\phi'(0)$ between charts, while the value of $u$ at any fixed
      modulus point is unchanged.
      This is the first-order companion of the genus covariance of Corollary~\ref{cor:reparam}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[Chart-independence of the saturation contact]
      \label{prop:contact-invariant}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence}.
      The first saturation contact $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy} is a geometric condition
      on the radicand: its solution is independent of the chart and of the coefficients of $\Bsat$ beyond the radicand
      itself.
      The locked split $u(s_*)$ is therefore invariant under orientation-preserving reparametrisation, and $|u(s_*)|$
      under every odd reparametrisation, the sign labelling the branch.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The contact is the level condition $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$; a level set of a function on the modulus line is a point of the
      manifold, carried covariantly by any reparametrisation, hence the same physical configuration in every chart.
      Evaluating the odd carrier $u$ there yields a chart-independent value; an orientation-reversing chart exchanges
      the two members $\pm s_*$ of the level set and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}, flips the sign of $u$ while preserving
      $|u(s_*)|$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Boundary contact is the unique admissible lock]
      \label{cor:boundary-admissible}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, \ref{lem:coincidence} and~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}, and a purely
      electric supplied two-form.
      Within the no-circularity discipline --- only data derived in the present corpus may enter, with no non-derived
      complex phase or cascade ordering --- the saturation contact $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$ is the unique chart-independent A4 lock.
      The interior candidate of Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy} is located by the sixth-order coefficient of the
      radicand, equivalently by the complex metaplectic phase and the cascade ordering left non-derived in the Conditional
      paragraph of Section~\ref{sec:status}; a finite truncation of the radicand has, moreover, a chart-dependent extremum.
      The interior lock is therefore inadmissible unless that phase and ordering are supplied.
    \end{corollary}

    This upgrades the coordinate-level dichotomy of Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy} to an admissibility-level
    selection: the boundary contact is selected not only on the real cascade of the model (path-invariance,
    Remark~\ref{rem:path-invariant}) but as the unique fence-admissible lock, independent of any chart.
    The remaining magnitude $|u| = |u(s_*)|$ is thereby reduced, within the fence, to the value of the split at the
    contact, sourced at first order, in a covariant family, by the $\JPi$-odd tangent of the eliminated block together
    with the first-order motion of the spinorial embedding~\cite{Beau2026ebj} and dressed by the contact displacement
    $s_*$, with the single honest caveat that a future first-principles derivation of the complex phase and the
    ordering could reopen an interior competitor.

    \paragraph{Genus (this note).}
      The genus is the sign of $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}$ for a supplied two-form
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:index-assignment});
      it is not determined here.
      The central bracket does not supply an electric assignment (Remark~\ref{rem:bracket}): the central direction is
      not
      temporal in the co-metrics of the corpus, the coefficient of the $\Jthree$-signal comes from the
      $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator, and no map from the generation operator to a two-form is supplied.
      For a supplied purely electric two-form, $\muchi < 0$, the symmetric point is unstable and, under
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}, the split is non-zero at the saturated pair; for a purely magnetic one,
      $\muchi > 0$ and $u = 0$.
      On the real symplectic cascade of the model the transverse (spin-two) cubic response vanishes path-invariantly at

    \paragraph{Conditional (this note).}
      The sixth-order interior lock --- a $\Pchi$-transverse cubic response, equivalently a non-empty spin-two
      channel --- is doubly conditional on two independent inputs the corpus does not fix.

    \paragraph{Selection (this note, Section~\ref{sec:boundary}).}
      Under the premises above and for a purely electric supplied two-form, within the no-circularity fence the

      contact.
      A future first-principles derivation of that phase and ordering could reopen an interior competitor.

    \paragraph{Front D factorisation (conditional).}
      Under the localisation hypothesis that the first-order source of the split is exhausted by the transported
      left-handed block $\tfrac12 \partial_s \Dchi(P)|_0$ of the $\JPi$-odd tangent, that is, that the $J_3$ component
      of the first-order residue $E_1$ of~\cite{Beau2026ebj} coincides with that of the auxiliary compression
      $E_1^{\mathrm{aux}} = -G_0\,\dot Q(0)\,G_0^{*}$, $G_0 = \Pi_S(0)\,D$ the finite embedding composed with the Dirac
      operator, the two embedding-motion terms contributing nothing to it, and that within the compression the
      chirally off-diagonal part of the odd tangent contributes nothing either, the contact split
      $u_\partial = u(s_*)$ of a covariant family factorises into three separated normalisations:
      \[
        u_\partial = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma\,s_*\,\frac{T_{J_3}\bigl(\partial_s \Dchi(P)|_0\bigr)}{\langle J_3, J_3\rangle}.
      \]
      Here $\sqrt{2}\,\sigma$ is the even-block factor, from the algebraic value
      $E_0^2|_{\Cgen} = \diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, with $\sigma = \pm1$ the branch sign;
      $s_*$ is the radial A4 contact fixed by $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$, carrying the dependence on the
      $\mathcal{R}_\chi$-transverse curvature of the two-form trajectory; and $T_{J_3}$ is the oriented Cartan projection
      of the $\mathcal{D}^{\pm}$-transported chiral defect rate, the angular symplectic-area observable of the
      oriented-frontier thread --- a closed linear functional evaluated on the defect rate, the factor one half of the
      block identity being absorbed in its normalisation, not a positive norm --- whose normalised evaluation is
      governed by the dictionary-bound frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$.
      The hypothesis is not established in~\cite{Beau2026ebj}, which determines one block of the odd tangent, not a
      unique carrier, and whose covariant realisation has a split rate sourced also by the first-order motion of the
      embedding and different from the rate of the auxiliary compression, which is not the residue, so that the
      hypothesis fails in that family and can hold only as a property of the family selected by the programme; the
      selection of the family and the localisation of the odd tangent onto its left-handed block remain open there.
      Under the hypothesis the remaining magnitude data are the two upstream quantities already isolated --- the radial
      contact $s_*$ through the A4 radicand curvature, and the angular normalisation of $T_{J_3}$ through
      $\mathcal{N}_A$ --- so the numerical magnitude remains upstream and dictionary-bound.

    \paragraph{Radial contact (Front 1).}
      The radial contact $s_*$ is fixed in closed form by Corollary~\ref{cor:radial-contact}: for a supplied
      purely electric two-form, in the affine electric gauge $s_* = \beta/|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|$.
      The quartic correction is entirely the transverse Schur backreaction $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$, whose
      $\Pchi$-parallel part is removed by odd reparametrisation (Remark~\ref{rem:conditional-sign}).
      The radial contact $s_*$ is thereby stated as a form result, the radial factor of the contact split under the
      conditional factorisation above, while its numerical magnitude still
      routes through the upstream normalisations $\beta$, $|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|$, and $\mathcal{N}_A$.

    \paragraph{Open.}
      The amplitude mechanism is stated (saturation, Genus above); the magnitude $|u|$ is narrowed by
      Section~\ref{sec:amplitude} but not closed.
      On the real cascade of the model the boundary-versus-interior selection is resolved in favour of saturation, so
      the
      remaining magnitude datum is the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ that maps the locked
      modulus $s_*$ to $|u|$, the cubic response $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ surviving only modulo odd reparametrisation.
      Equivalently, the open datum is the $\Pchi$-transverse curvature of the two-form trajectory $\{\Fchi(s)\}$, the
      part of $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ that fixes the amplitude beyond the genus (informally the torsion of the projector
      path; Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:fixed-generator-orbit} sets it to zero), together with the saturation-wall test
      $\Dchi(s_*) > 0$ and $\mathcal{N}_A$.
      The cubic response is understood modulo odd reparametrisation of the local modulus: under
      $s = t + \alpha t^3 + O(t^5)$ one has
      \[
        \mathcal{R}_{\chi} \mapsto \mathcal{R}_{\chi} + \alpha\, \Pchi ,
      \]
      so the $\Pchi$-parallel component of $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is a parametrisation gauge, while the physical datum is
      the curvature of the two-form trajectory together with $\mathcal{N}_A$ mapping the locked modulus to $|u|$.
      The residual amplitude problem is therefore not a parametrisation artefact: the genus is
      reparametrisation-invariant, and the magnitude is fixed only by the invariant A4 matching of the saturated
      two-form curve with $\mathcal{N}_A$.
      This is downstream of the genus and is not a genus question: the genus is not determined here
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:index-assignment}), and, for a supplied two-form, the magnitude requires the cubic datum and the
      normalisation.
      In the Schur-null branch the quadratic term vanishes and the locking character falls to the marginal branch of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}, decided by the leading even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$ rather than by the genus.

      The normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ is itself dictionary-bound, not a parameter-free prediction.
      It is the angular analogue of the radial scale $\kappa = 5/12$ of the exit-deficit dictionary~\cite{Beau2026aar,
    Beau2026q14}, which fixes the split value $\varepsilon = 1/10$ by matching the derived three-level deficit structure
      to the external ADE ratio $3{:}2$ of a selected case, one of four three-level cases of differing
      ratios~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
      The first-principles status of $\varepsilon = 1/10$ is therefore exactly that of the ADE case-selection gate, and
      for the present corpus construction that gate is a no-go: its obstruction is isolated in
      AOG-Note~\cite{Beau2026aog} as an arithmetic orthogonality between Born--Infeld parity and the Galois-spin action,
      established by a character-table audit of $2I \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,5)$.
      The residual frequency reflection $R_b = W(-I)$ is the central Weil lift, and its commutation with $\gamma_5$
      requires a Weil-to-spin group map that no source supplies~\cite{Beau2026q14}; under that map the chiral oriented
      signal that would carry $\mathcal{N}_A$ is defined and its integrand survives the symmetric-frontier cancellation
      that annihilates the bare cocycle~\cite{Beau2026q11of, Beau2026aar}; but evaluating it and comparing to
      $\varepsilon = 1/10$ would be a dictionary matching, not a test,
      since $1/10$ is a dictionary value and $\mathcal{N}_A$ its conversion factor.
      The genuine first-principles front is therefore upstream and structural, not a numerical amplitude: the ADE case
      selection and the level-to-generation map, the derivation of the projective-resolution growth
      $\Lambda_{\mathrm{proj}}(n)$ and of the cascade exponent $\beta$ (the central open problem
      of~\cite{Beau2026a4}; no structural bound on $\beta$ is established, the Born--Infeld/Ramanujan route not
      yielding one~\cite{Beau2026a8}), and the derivation of the transfer constant $N_{\mathrm{casc}}$ that
      fixes $\mathcal{N}_A$~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
