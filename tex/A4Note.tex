  \begin{abstract}
    This companion of the Projective Residue Schur reduction (PRS) examines the Lorentzian saturation functional
    controlling the generation split \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u}.
    The symmetric square of the \texorpdfstring{$\JPi$}{J_Π}-odd modulus is \texorpdfstring{$\JPi$}{J_Π}-even and
    cannot carry the branch
    sign; the carrier is taken, by hypothesis, to be an odd antisymmetric chiral two-form
    \texorpdfstring{$\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$}{F_{μν}(s)}.
    \texorpdfstring{$\Bsat(s)$}{B_sat(s)} is defined as the Born--Infeld determinantal saturation margin of such a
    supplied
    two-form, and its second variation
    \texorpdfstring{$\muchi = \tfrac12 \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}$}{μ_χ² = ½ P_{μν} P^{μν}} is the Lorentzian genus
    of the chiral
    polarisation \texorpdfstring{$\Pchi$}{P}.
    Hypotheses that no source supplies are stated where used: a Lorentzian co-metric, the construction of the two-form,
    a local-minimum reading of A4, the existence and non-degeneracy of a locked pair, the covariance of the locking
    family under \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{J_Π}, and, from PRS, its generation-reading and first-order transport
    hypotheses.
    The scope is that of PRS: the split vanishes for the reference operator, the consequences for a general locking
    operator are conditional, and no mechanism for a non-zero split is derived.
    The genus is not determined: the central commutator supplies no electric assignment, since the central direction is
    not temporal in the co-metrics of the corpus and no map from the generation operator to a two-form is available.
    The quartic coefficient of the radicand depends on the parametrisation and selects no lock; an exactly straight
    purely electric ray reaches the bound at \texorpdfstring{$t_*=\beta/|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|$}{t* = β/|E|}.
    The magnitude \texorpdfstring{$|u|$}{|u|} is not predicted.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position and purpose}
    \label{sec:position}

    The fermionic sub-programme locates the three-generation mass split in the $\Jthree$-odd part of the squared projective
    endomorphism $\Epi^2$ restricted to the gauge-singlet generation triplet $\Cgen$, parametrised by a single real number
    $u$ through $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen} = \diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, with the even sector
    $\diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, the algebraic value of $(C_2-J_3^2)/C_2$ at $C_2=2$.
    Its reading as the Born--Infeld even sector would require an identification between the
    conditional $3\times3$ model of~\cite{Beau2026a34} on $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$ and
    $(E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}$; no such identification is available, so that reading is not used
    here.
    The Schur reduction~\cite{Beau2026prs} relates $u$ to the antiunitary chiral equivariance defect
    $\Delta_\chi(P) = \pi_{LL} - \tau \pi_{RR} \tau^{-1}$ of the eliminated block $1 - P$ as a structural reading,
    not a theorem; under its generation-reading hypothesis, a non-zero $u$ requires a locking operator that breaks
    $\JPi$-equivariance, and on its interpretive reading of the axioms this can come only from axiom A4.
    For the finite locking operator, $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$ holds in~\cite{Beau2026prs} only under an identification of
    the parity $c\leftrightarrow q-c$ with $\JPi$, an equivariant construction and a finite reading of the Schur
    expression, none of which is supplied; a non-zero split would then need a $\JPi$-odd datum outside that
    construction, located in the Lorentzian completion as a hypothesis.
    The split is then governed by the chiral curvature $\muchi := \partial_s^2 \Bsat(0)$ of a saturation functional $\Bsat$
    along the $\JPi$-odd modulus $s$ opened by Lorentzian complexification, with the trichotomy: $\muchi > 0$ makes the
    symmetric point a stable minimum of $\Bsat$; $\muchi < 0$ makes it an unstable point, and a $\JPi$-conjugate
    locked pair $\pm s_*$ with a split $u \neq 0$ is then a hypothesis (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate});
    $\muchi = 0$ is marginal.

    This note states the structural prerequisites for $\muchi$ and states the premises under which its sign is read.
    Section~\ref{sec:oriented} records that the modulus is $\JPi$-odd and oriented, and recalls the oriented frontier
    observable.
    Section~\ref{sec:antisymmetry} proves that the symmetric square $M = \Fchi \Fchi$ cannot carry the sign of the
    $\JPi$-odd modulus, and takes an odd antisymmetric two-form $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$ as the carrier by hypothesis.
    Section~\ref{sec:margin} defines $\Bsat(s)$ as the Born--Infeld determinantal saturation margin of $\Fchi$, fixes the
    sign
    convention by its admissibility role, and isolates the convention trap explicitly.
    Section~\ref{sec:reduction} reduces the second variation to a Lorentzian norm.
    Section~\ref{sec:coincidence} relates the margin to the Schur tilt and states the coincidence with the
    Born--Infeld modulus as a hypothesis.
    Section~\ref{sec:genus} examines the genus of the chiral polarisation: the central commutator does not supply an
    electric assignment, and the genus of the supplied two-form is not determined.
    Section~\ref{sec:amplitude} extends the radicand to quartic order, shows that the quartic coefficient is
    parametrisation-dependent, and states what an exactly straight electric ray gives.
    Section~\ref{sec:boundary} records the chart covariance of the defect rate and of the first contact; no selection
    between a boundary contact and an interior stationary point is established.
    Section~\ref{sec:status} records the epistemic strata.

    We work throughout on a flat effective co-metric of Lorentzian signature, on which the operator-level and
    spectral-level definitions of $u$ coincide~\cite{Beau2026prs}.
    Only the signature enters the genus.
    The sources leave the coefficients and the signature of a full-rank co-metric conditional: the rank-three
    co-metric of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} has a zero central entry, the completion of Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} is conditional,
    and the temporal coefficient is unconstrained~\cite{Beau2026q11}.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Lorentzian effective co-metric, {[H-Lor]}]
      \label{hyp:lorentzian}
      The effective co-metric is a full-rank four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric of signature $(-,+,+,+)$.
      Only the signature enters the genus.
      No source supplies it: Q5b has rank three, Q8 is conditional, and Q11 gives this signature only for a negative
      temporal and positive spatial coefficient.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \paragraph{Scope.}
    This note takes over the scope of~\cite{Beau2026prs}: for the reference operator $\Pi_S^*\Pi_S$ the split vanishes
    under the generation-reading hypothesis; for a general locking operator $P\neq\Pi_S^*\Pi_S$ the

  \section{The chiral modulus is $\JPi$-odd and oriented}
    \label{sec:oriented}

    The spinorial Born--Infeld parity lift is the antiunitary structure $\JPi = \epsilon \circ \mathrm{conj}$ with
    $\epsilon = i\sigma_y$, giving $\JPi^2 = -1$ on the fundamental doublet~\cite{Beau2026q14, Beau2026prs}.
    On the generation triplet $\Cgen = \mathrm{span}(e_0, e_+, e_-)$ the induced involution $\Jtwo$ satisfies
    $(\Jtwo)^2 = +1$, with $\Jtwo e_0 = -e_0$ and $\Jtwo e_\pm = e_\mp$, and consequently
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Jtwo-Jthree}
      \Jtwo \, \Jthree \, (\Jtwo)^{-1} = -\Jthree ,
    \end{equation}
    so that the Born--Infeld conjugation exchanges the two outer generation weights~\cite{Beau2026prs}.

    \begin{lemma}[Oddness of the split]
      \label{lem:u-odd}
      Let $s$ be the $\JPi$-odd modulus, with $\JPi : s \mapsto -s$.
      Assume the generation-reading hypothesis of~\cite{Beau2026prs} (its Hypothesis [H-Gen], with $J_\Pi$-compatible
      $D$ and $\Pi_S$ and a reading map to the generation space) and that the family
      $\Epi^2(s)|_{\Cgen}=\diag(1,\tfrac12+u(s),\tfrac12-u(s))$ is
      $\JPi$-covariant, $\Jtwo\,\Epi^2(s)\,(\Jtwo)^{-1}=\Epi^2(-s)$; this holds when moreover
      $P(-s)=\JPi P(s)\JPi^{-1}$, an assumption of this note that PRS does not supply.
      Then $u = u(s)$ is an odd function, $u(-s) = -u(s)$, and in particular $u(0) = 0$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The triplet endomorphism $\Epi^2|_{\Cgen} = \diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ is written in the weight basis
      $(e_0, e_+, e_-)$.
      Since $\Jtwo$ swaps $e_+ \leftrightarrow e_-$ by~\eqref{eq:Jtwo-Jthree}, conjugation by $\Jtwo$ sends this endomorphism
      to
      $\diag(1, \tfrac12 - u, \tfrac12 + u)$, i.e.\ $u \mapsto -u$.
      By the assumed covariance $\Epi^2(-s)|_{\Cgen}=\diag(1,\tfrac12-u(s),\tfrac12+u(s))$, that is $u(-s)=-u(s)$,
      and $u(0)=0$ follows.
    \end{proof}

    The orientation carried by the modulus is not an artefact of the chiral frame.
    The oriented frontier observable of~\cite{Beau2026q11of} is a discrete counterpart of the orientation datum, not of
    $u$ (PRS shows that no finite front observable carries $u$): on the directed outgoing edges
    $g \to gs$ of the cascade the central increment is the Heisenberg cocycle
    $\Delta A_c(g, s) = c\,(z(s) + a(g) s_b)$, reduced to $c\,a(g) s_b$ on admissible generators, which is the discrete
    oriented
    increment of the cascade (the $\Jthree$-coefficient of the metaplectic step comes from the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$
    commutator, not from the Heisenberg bracket, Q14 Proposition~6.5).
    This increment is linear in the orientation datum $s_b$ and therefore odd under orientation reversal.
    Its symmetric average vanishes identically, $\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0$ on symmetric shells~\cite{Beau2026aar},
    which is exactly the statement that a symmetrisation annihilates an orientation-odd signal.

  \section{Antisymmetric structure of the chiral modulus}
    \label{sec:antisymmetry}

    We now show that the symmetric square of an odd two-form cannot carry the branch sign of the $\JPi$-odd modulus.
    Let $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$ be a candidate two-form built from the chiral-odd deformation, with the linear expansion
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Fchi-linear}
      \Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s) = s\,\Pchi_{\mu\nu} + O(s^2), \qquad \Pchi := \partial_s \Fchi |_{s=0} ,
    \end{equation}
    and let
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Mdef}
      M^{\mu}{}_{\nu}(s) := \Fchi^{\mu\rho}(s)\, \Fchi_{\rho\nu}(s)
    \end{equation}
    be the symmetric combination obtained by squaring it.
    The antiunitary lift acts on the two-form by $\JPi : \Fchi(s) \mapsto \Fchi(-s)$, that is by $\Fchi \mapsto -\Fchi$ at
    linear order in $s$, consistently with Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}.

    \begin{lemma}[Antisymmetric carrier]
      \label{lem:antisymmetry}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      The symmetric square $M(s)$ is $\JPi$-even and stationary at the symmetric point:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:M-even}
        M(-s) = M(s), \qquad M(0) = 0, \qquad \partial_s M(0) = 0 .
      \end{equation}
      Consequently every scalar functional $\Phi[M]$ is even in $s$ to the order at which $M$ is even and obeys
      $\partial_s \Phi[M]|_{s=0} = 0$; it assigns the same value to the two $\JPi$-conjugate branches $\pm s_*$ and is
      therefore
      blind to the sign of $u$.
      The split modulus cannot be carried by $M$.
      A variable that resolves the two branches must change sign under $s \mapsto -s$; the odd antisymmetric two-form
      of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} does.
      This note does not show that it is the only odd carrier.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, $\Fchi(-s) = -\Fchi(s)$, so by~\eqref{eq:Mdef}
      $M = \Fchi \Fchi \mapsto (-\Fchi)(-\Fchi) = M$: the square is $\JPi$-invariant.
      Substituting~\eqref{eq:Fchi-linear} gives $M(s) = s^2\, \Pchi^{\mu\rho} \Pchi_{\rho\nu} + O(s^3)$, whence
      $M(0) = 0$ and $\partial_s M(0) = 0$, which is~\eqref{eq:M-even}.
      For any functional $\Phi[M]$ the chain rule gives
      $\partial_s \Phi[M]|_{0} = (\delta\Phi/\delta M) : \partial_s M|_{0} = 0$, so $\Phi$ has no linear response to the
      modulus.
      The branch choice is the sign of $u(s_*) = -u(-s_*)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}; distinguishing $+s_*$ from
      $-s_*$ requires a quantity odd in $s$, which $\Phi[M]$ is not.
      Therefore $M$ cannot be the primary modulus variable.
      The odd two-form of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} changes sign under $s \mapsto -s$, as the
      orientation-odd increment of the discrete frontier observable~\cite{Beau2026q11of, Beau2026aar} does.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Role of the symmetric square]
      \label{cor:M-backreaction}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      The symmetric combination $M = \Fchi \Fchi$ enters the saturation functional only at second order in $s$, as a
      $\JPi$-even saturating backreaction or higher-order term.
      It is excluded as the primary modulus variable but admissible as a subleading contribution.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By~\eqref{eq:M-even} the leading occurrence of $M$ is $O(s^2)$ and $\JPi$-even, which is the order and parity of a
      backreaction term rather than of the linear modulus carrier of Lemma~\ref{lem:antisymmetry}.
    \end{proof}

    The lemma bears on the antisymmetric/symmetric fork implicit in the definition deliverable of~\cite{Beau2026prs}:
    the symmetric replacement $M^{\mu}{}_{\nu} = \Fchi^{\mu\rho} \Fchi_{\rho\nu}$
    used
    elsewhere to remove a Pfaffian sign ambiguity is precisely what must be avoided at the level of the primary variable,
    because removing the sign ambiguity removes the orientation that carries the split.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Construction of the chiral two-form, {[H-F]}]
      \label{hyp:chiral-two-form}
      Let $\mathcal J$ be the real vector space of Hermitian operators $X$ on $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$.
      with $\JPi X\JPi^{-1}=-X$, $\JPi$ being antiunitary and exchanging $e_+$ and $e_-$.
      Every such operator is a real multiple of $\Jthree|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}$, so $\mathcal J=\mathbb R\,\Jthree$
      (the off-diagonal Hermitian operators are $\JPi$-even).
      There is a real-linear map $\iota:\mathcal J\to\{\text{real two-forms of the effective spacetime}\}$, injective,
      that
      is $F_0:=\iota(\Jthree)\neq0$.
      For the locking operators $P(s)$ with $P(-s)=\JPi P(s)\JPi^{-1}$, write the $\JPi$-odd part of
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(P(s)-P(0))$ of~\cite{Beau2026prs} as $\varphi(s)\,\Jthree$, with $\varphi$ real and odd.
      The chiral two-form is $\Fchi(s):=\iota(\varphi(s)\Jthree)=\varphi(s)\,F_0$, exactly odd, $\Fchi(-s)=-\Fchi(s)$,
      with $\Pchi=\varphi'(0)F_0$ and $\Fchi(s)=s\,\Pchi+O(s^3)$.
      Here $s$ is the Schur modulus of the locking operator.
      No source supplies $\iota$: the generation operator $\Jthree$ acts on the generation factor, a Clifford bivector
      acts on the spinor factor, and the two carriers are distinct.
      It is a missing element, not an excluded one.
      The domain $\mathcal J$ is one-dimensional, the codomain is the space of real two-forms, and injectivity means
      $F_0\neq0$; no equivariance of $\iota$ is assumed or possible, since $\mathcal J$ is not invariant under the
      generation $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{remark}[Collinearity of the trajectory]
      \label{rem:collinear}
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the trajectory $\{\Fchi(s)\}$ lies on the line $\mathbb R F_0$:
      $\Fchi(s)=\varphi(s)F_0$.
      Consequently $\mathcal R_\chi=\tfrac{\varphi'''(0)}{6}F_0$ is parallel to $\Pchi$ and, when
      $\Pchi\cdot\Pchi\neq0$,
      $\mathcal R_{\chi,\perp}=0$
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}).
      This is a consequence of restricting the carrier to the $\JPi$-odd Hermitian part on the outer weights; a
      non-collinear trajectory would require a carrier with a larger domain, which Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}
      does not provide.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Non-degeneracy of the split at the saturated pair, {[H-Nd]}]
      \label{hyp:nondegenerate}
      When $\muchi<0$, the radicand $\Dchi$ reaches its bound at a finite contact $s_*>0$, which gives a
      $\JPi$-conjugate saturated pair $\pm s_*$ of the locking, and the odd carrier $u(s)$ of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd} does not vanish at that pair.
      Oddness gives $u(0)=0$ and $u(-s)=-u(s)$ only.
    \end{hypothesis}

  \section{The Born--Infeld saturation margin}
    \label{sec:margin}

    We now define the functional whose chiral curvature governs the split.
    Let $g_{\mu\nu}$ be an effective metric of Lorentzian signature and let $\beta$ be the
    Born--Infeld saturation scale of the bounded-flux completion~\cite{Beau2026c}.
    Born--Infeld is taken here as the determinantal completion carried by an antisymmetric field strength; the
    source~\cite{Beau2026c} presents it as a conditional completion, neither derived nor unique, and does not derive the
    antisymmetric field strength.
    It is an input of this note, into which the antisymmetric two-form $\Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)$ of
    Section~\ref{sec:antisymmetry} enters.

    \begin{definition}[Chiral admissibility radicand and saturation margin]
      \label{def:B}
      Let
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:Dchi}
        \Dchi(s) := \frac{-\det\!\big(g_{\mu\nu} + \beta^{-1} \Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s)\big)}{-\det g_{\mu\nu}} ,
      \end{equation}
      the dimensionless ratio in which the metric determinant cancels.
      Admissibility of the chiral-odd deformation is the reality and non-negativity of the radicand, $\Dchi(s) \geq 0$, and
      saturation is its approach to the bound $\Dchi(s) \to 0$.
      The Born--Infeld saturation margin along the modulus is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:B}
        \Bsat(s) := \beta^2 \left( \sqrt{\Dchi(s)} - 1 \right) .
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    The functional $\Bsat$ is fixed in its role, not merely in its formula.
    By the projection-locking theorem~\cite{Beau2026a26} and axiom A4~\cite{Beau2026m}, resolution occurs only where the
    continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the discrete shell support; the physically locked chiral configuration is the
    saturated admissible extremum selected on that support.
    The variational reading is stated as a hypothesis.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Local-minimum reading of A4, {[H-Min]}]
      \label{hyp:local-min}
      Axiom A4 locks the modulus at a local minimum of $\Bsat$ along $s$, among the admissible values,
      $\Dchi(s)\ge0$.
      No source states it.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{remark}[The convention sign-trap]
      \label{rem:trap}
      The determinantal scalar~\eqref{eq:B} admits an overall sign ambiguity according to the object it names.
      Read as the Born--Infeld action density, it is extremised by the equations of motion and recovers a Maxwell-type
      weak-field expansion; read as the saturation margin, it is minimised at the locking event of A4.
      The two readings differ by an overall sign, and that sign propagates directly into $\partial_s^2 \Bsat(0)$.
      Choosing the sign so as to obtain a desired trichotomy would make the conclusion an artefact of convention rather than a
      derived result.
      We therefore fix the sign once, by the admissibility role of $\Bsat$ (Definition~\ref{def:B}, anchored
      in~\cite{Beau2026a26, Beau2026m}), independently of and prior to any knowledge of the sign of the second variation.
      With this fixing, the sign of the split is never inferred from the convention: it is carried solely by the Lorentzian
      genus of $\Pchi$ computed in Section~\ref{sec:reduction}.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{proposition}[Parity of the margin]
      \label{prop:parity}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      $\Bsat(s) = \Bsat(-s)$, so $s = 0$ is automatically a stationary point of $\Bsat$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the two-form obeys $\Fchi(-s) = -\Fchi(s)$, and
      the determinant
      in~\eqref{eq:Dchi}
      depends on $\Fchi$ only through even powers, since for an antisymmetric argument the odd elementary symmetric functions
      of
      $g^{-1}\Fchi$ vanish.
      Hence $\Dchi(s) = \Dchi(-s)$ and $\Bsat(s) = \Bsat(-s)$ by~\eqref{eq:B}.
      Evenness gives $\partial_s \Bsat(0) = 0$.
      This is the saturation-functional avatar of Born--Infeld parity, which acts covariantly
      on the BI response family, preserving even-order responses
      (O18 Proposition~2.6)~\cite{Beau2026a22}.
      The value $\diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ is algebraic; attributing it a Born--Infeld origin
      would require the identification declared unavailable above, and is not used here.
    \end{proof}

  \section{Second variation and the Lorentzian reduction}
    \label{sec:reduction}

    We expand the radicand to the order needed for the chiral curvature.
    The standard four-dimensional Born--Infeld determinant identity for an antisymmetric two-form gives
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:det-expansion}
      \Dchi(s) = 1 + \frac{1}{2\beta^2}\, \Fchi_{\mu\nu} \Fchi^{\mu\nu}
      - \frac{1}{16\beta^4}\, \big(\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Fchi}^{\mu\nu}\big)^2 ,
    \end{equation}
    where indices are raised with the effective co-metric and $\widetilde{\Fchi}$ is the Hodge dual.
    Substituting the linear expansion~\eqref{eq:Fchi-linear}, the pseudoscalar term is $O(s^4)$ and does not contribute to
    the
    second variation, so
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Dchi-quadratic}
      \Dchi(s) = 1 + \frac{s^2}{2\beta^2}\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} + O(s^4) .
    \end{equation}

    \begin{theorem}[Lorentzian reduction of the chiral curvature]
      \label{thm:reduction}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}; for the statements on $u$, assume also the
      premises of Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}.
      With $\Bsat$ as in Definition~\ref{def:B},
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:muchi}
        \muchi := \partial_s^2 \Bsat(0) = \tfrac12\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} ,
      \end{equation}
      so that $\operatorname{sign}(\muchi) = \operatorname{sign}\!\big(\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}\big)$, the Lorentzian
      genus of the chiral polarisation $\Pchi$ in the effective co-metric.
      A spacelike (magnetic-type) $\Pchi$ gives $\muchi > 0$, so that $s = 0$ is a strict local minimum of $\Bsat$;
      a timelike (electric-type) $\Pchi$ gives $\muchi < 0$, a strict local maximum; a null $\Pchi$ gives $\muchi = 0$,
      the marginal case, decided by the leading non-vanishing even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$ of
      $\Bsat(s) = \Bsat(0) + \lambda_{2k} s^{2k} + O(s^{2k+2})$.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:local-min}, if $\muchi<0$ the symmetric point is not a locked configuration, and a
      locked configuration, if one exists, lies at some $s\neq0$ and comes in a $\JPi$-conjugate pair $\pm s_*$;
      its existence and the non-vanishing of $u(\pm s_*)=\mp u(s_*)$ are the content of
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}.
      If $\muchi>0$, the symmetric point is a locked candidate with $u(0)=0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}); the theorem does
      not decide whether
      another locked configuration exists at larger $|s|$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      From~\eqref{eq:B} and~\eqref{eq:Dchi-quadratic},
      $\Bsat(s) = \beta^2 \big( \sqrt{1 + \tfrac{s^2}{2\beta^2} \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}} - 1 \big)
      = \tfrac{s^2}{4}\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} + O(s^4)$, giving~\eqref{eq:muchi}.
      In an orthonormal coframe of the Lorentzian co-metric the contraction $\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}$ is the
      Minkowski contraction, which for an antisymmetric two-form is
      $2(|\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}|^2 - |\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2)$,
      positive for a magnetic-type (spacelike-dominated) polarisation and negative for an electric-type
      (timelike-dominated) one.
      The sign of the second derivative of $\Bsat$ at the stationary point $s=0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}) gives
      the local-minimum or local-maximum statements.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:local-min}, a strict local maximum is not a locked configuration.
      A locked configuration at $s\neq0$ has a $\JPi$-conjugate partner because $\Bsat$ is even.
    \end{proof}

    For a purely magnetic ray $\Fchi(t)=t\,\Pchi$ with $\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}=0$ the radicand is exactly
    $\Dchi=1+t^2|\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}|^2/\beta^2$ and never reaches the bound; for a ray with
    $\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}\neq0$ the pseudoscalar term of~\eqref{eq:det-expansion} is
    negative and can bring $\Dchi$ to zero at finite $|s|$ even when $\muchi>0$.
    The statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} on $\muchi>0$ therefore concern a neighbourhood of $s=0$ only.

  \section{The coincidence lock}
    \label{sec:coincidence}

    The reduction of Section~\ref{sec:reduction} presupposes that the modulus entering the saturation
    margin~\eqref{eq:B} is tied to the $\JPi$-odd tilt of the eliminated Schur block.
    A priori these are two distinct quantities: the projector tilt $s_{\mathrm{proj}}$, defined at the level of the
    projection algebra through the deformation $1 - P(s)$ of the locking operator $P(s)$ (a family with $0\preceq
    P(s)\preceq1$
    and $P(0)=\Pi_S^*\Pi_S$, in general different from $\Pi_S^*\Pi_S$ for $s\neq0$), and the
    saturation
    modulus $s_{\mathrm{BI}}$, the chiral-odd companion of the Higgs amplitude mode of the saturated condensate.
    We motivate the lock by transposing the projection-locking theorem to the $\JPi$-odd sector.

    The axiom A4 is an intersection scheme, not a saturation criterion~\cite{Beau2026m, Beau2026a26}:
    resolution occurs where the continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the available discrete support of the projection,
    and the transition becomes structurally discrete.
    Transposed to the $\JPi$-odd sector, a continuous chiral-odd modulus is observable only if it is encoded by the
    projection.
    Since the observable space is $\mathcal{O} = \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$ and the Schur complement
    $\Epi = -\Pi_S D (1 - P) D \Pi_S^*$ sources the chiral structure through the eliminated block $1 - P$, a chiral-odd
    Born--Infeld deformation is realisable only as a tilt of the complexified block $1 - P^{\mathbb{C}}$, and conversely a
    tilt of $1 - P^{\mathbb{C}}$ is dynamically present only on the saturation locus.
    On this reading the two moduli are not independent on the locus.
    By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the margin $\Bsat$ is already a function of the Schur modulus, so
    Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} uses no second modulus; the coincidence below is needed only to read the A4 lock along
    the Born--Infeld modulus $s_{\mathrm{BI}}$ instead.
    At the finite level, the $\JPi$-odd part of such a tilt would vanish for a family of finite locking operators
    constructed equivariantly; PRS defines no such family and supplies no finite reading~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so the
    modulus is taken, as a hypothesis, to be carried by the Lorentzian completion.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Tangential coincidence on the A4 locus, {[H-Coin]}]
      \label{lem:coincidence}
      On the chiral-odd A4 locking locus the Born--Infeld modulus and the Schur tilt are related by a non-degenerate
      reparametrisation,
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:coincidence}
        s_{\mathrm{BI}} = \psi(s_{\mathrm{proj}}), \qquad \psi(0) = 0, \qquad \psi'(0) \neq 0 ,
      \end{equation}
      so that A4 identifies their first-order transverse direction up to non-degenerate reparametrisation.
      This is a tangential identification of the $\JPi$-odd direction, not a strict identification of the parameters.
      It is not derived from the Schur transversality or from the projection-locking theorem.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{corollary}[Reparametrisation-invariance of the genus]
      \label{cor:reparam}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, and let $\psi$ be any $C^2$
      reparametrisation
      of the modulus with $\psi(0)=0$ and $\psi'(0)\neq0$, in particular that of Hypothesis~\ref{lem:coincidence}.
      Then
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:reparam}
        \partial_{s}^2 (\Bsat\circ\psi)(0) = \psi'(0)^2 \, \partial_{s}^2 \Bsat(0) ,
      \end{equation}
      so $\operatorname{sign}(\muchi)$ is independent of the chart, and the genus reduction of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is well-posed.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Equation~\eqref{eq:reparam} is the chain rule for $\Bsat \circ \psi$ at $\psi(0) = 0$, using $\partial_s \Bsat(0)
      = 0$
      from Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}; the positive factor $\psi'(0)^2$ leaves the sign unchanged.
    \end{proof}

    The first-order projector variation supplies the chiral polarisation explicitly.
    Differentiating $\Epi(s) = -\Pi_S D (1 - P(s)) D \Pi_S^*$ at $s = 0$ with $D$ and $\Pi_S$ held fixed, and using
    $\partial_s [-(1 - P(s))] = \partial_s P(s)$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Pchi-schur}
      \partial_s \Epi|_{0} = \Pi_S D (\partial_s P|_{0}) D \Pi_S^* ,
      \qquad
      \Pchi = \iota\Big(\Pi_S D (\partial_s P|_{0}) D \Pi_S^* \big|_{\mathrm{out},\,\JPi\text{-odd}}\Big) .
    \end{equation}
    Here the argument of $\iota$ is the $\JPi$-odd Hermitian part, on the outer weights $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$, of the
    Schur transport (an element of $\mathcal J$ of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}).
    The positive sign is the derivative of $-(1 - P)$, not a hidden conventional choice; it is fixed by the Schur form, in
    keeping with the convention discipline of Section~\ref{sec:margin}.

    \begin{definition}[Schur transversality on the $\JPi$-odd eliminated sector]
      \label{def:transversality}
      Under the $\JPi$-compatibility of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, let $\sigma$ be the $\JPi$-odd part of
      $\partial_s P|_{0}$ and $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)=\big(\Pi_S D\sigma D\Pi_S^*\big)\big|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}$
      its
      Schur transport restricted to the outer weights.
      The locking is Schur-transverse if
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:transversality}
        T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\neq 0 ,
      \end{equation}
      and Schur-null otherwise; this is the notion of PRS, and $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ is the $\JPi$-odd part of the
      first-order polarisation on the outer weights.
    \end{definition}

    The Schur-null and Schur-transverse loci translate to the A4 locus as follows.

    \begin{proposition}[Schur-null and Schur-transverse loci on the A4 locus]
      \label{prop:dichotomy}
      The identification of the transverse and null loci is established at operator level in the Schur
      reduction~\cite{Beau2026prs}
      under the hypotheses stated there; transposed to the A4 locus through Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}
      ($\iota$ linear and injective, with $\Pchi=\iota(T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma))$ and the $\JPi$-compatibility of PRS)
      it reads as follows.
      On the chiral-odd A4 locus:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item if the locking is Schur-transverse, then $\Pchi\neq0$ by the injectivity of $\iota$, and the reduction
        $\muchi = \tfrac12 \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is the genus computation;
        \item if the locking is Schur-null, then $\Pchi = 0$ and $\muchi = 0$ at quadratic order, the marginal branch
        of the trichotomy of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}, decided by the first non-vanishing even coefficient
        $\lambda_{2k}$ rather than directly by $u = 0$.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{proposition}

    PRS~\cite{Beau2026prs} identifies the Schur-null and Schur-transverse loci under explicit hypotheses and leaves
    the branch open: the oriented metaplectic step has the exact $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ opening
    $\alpha(t,s)=ts\,r/\sinh r\not\equiv0$ with $\mu(t,s)\equiv0$, and its transport to $\partial_s P|_{0}$ is the
    transport hypothesis [H-Tr] of PRS.
    By Corollary~\ref{cor:reparam} the genus does not depend on the positive Jacobian $\psi'(0)$.

  \section{The genus of the chiral polarisation}
    \label{sec:genus}

    The genus of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is the sign of $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}$, fixed by the index structure
    of the chiral polarisation~\eqref{eq:Pchi-schur} once a two-form carrier is supplied
    (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}).
    In the regime where the compression remainder of~\cite{Beau2026prs} is the projective endomorphism
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14},
    the projected Dirac square has the Lichnerowicz form
    $E=\tfrac{R}{4}+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu}T^a+\Epi$~\cite{Beau2026q14, Beau2026prs}; its bivector
    term is the gauge curvature, a summand distinct from $\Epi$, so no source writes the chiral polarisation as a
    Clifford bivector.
    For a supplied two-form the electric and magnetic parts are $\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P, i} = \Pchi_{0i}$ and
    $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P, i} = \tfrac12 \epsilon_{ijk} \Pchi_{jk}$ in an orthonormal coframe, the decomposition used
    in Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}.

    \begin{lemma}[Genus of a supplied chiral two-form]
      \label{lem:index-assignment}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      In an orthonormal coframe of the effective co-metric,
      $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}=2\big(|\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}|^2-|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2\big)$ and
      $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu}\propto\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}$.
      Hence a purely electric two-form, $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}=0\neq\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}$, has
      $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}=-2|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2<0$, $\muchi<0$ and vanishing pseudoscalar, and a
      purely
      magnetic one has $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}>0$ and $\muchi>0$.
      The data of this note do not determine which of $\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}$, $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P}$ is non-zero.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      These are the standard decompositions of an antisymmetric two-form in an orthonormal Lorentzian coframe.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The central bracket does not supply an electric assignment]
      \label{rem:bracket}
      The Lie bracket $[X, Y] = Z$ is the algebraic generation of the central direction from the horizontal plane, not
      its metric index.
      An electric assignment of the $\Jthree$-odd signal would pair the central direction with $\gamma^0$, that is
      identify $\partial_\tau$ with $Z$.
      In the co-metric of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} (Theorem~5.2) the central entry is zero; in the conditional
      completion of Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} (Corollary~6.1) it is positive; in Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} $\tau$ is a separate
      factor with a free coefficient.
      The central direction is temporal in none of them.
      Identifying $\partial_\tau$ with $Z$ would abandon the product structure of Q5b (Remark~3.3) and is a new
      geometric proposal, not an inherited identification; keeping $\tau$ temporal while making $Z$ temporal would give
      two temporal directions.
      The coefficient $\alpha$ of the $\Jthree$-signal is produced by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator $[E,F]=H$ of
      the two shear generators (Q14, Proposition~6.5); the Heisenberg bracket does not appear in that computation.
      Finally, no map from $\Jthree\in\mathrm{End}(\Cgen)$ to the components of a two-form is supplied
      (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}).
      Neither an electric nor a magnetic genus therefore follows from the central bracket.
    \end{remark}

    The genus deferred in Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is not determined here.
    For a supplied purely electric two-form, the amplitude analysis of Section~\ref{sec:amplitude} applies, and what
    remains is the amplitude $|u|$, the A4 matching of the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation with $\muchi$, which
    is not a genus question.

  \section{The amplitude frontier}
    \label{sec:amplitude}

    The genus fixes the sign of $\muchi$; it does not fix the magnitude $|u|$.
    This section extends the radicand beyond the second variation for a supplied two-form.
    Because $\Bsat$ is a monotone function of $\Dchi$ through~\eqref{eq:B}, the stationary points of $\Bsat$ and of
    $\Dchi$ coincide.

    By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the two-form is an odd function of the modulus, refining the linear
    expansion~\eqref{eq:Fchi-linear} to
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Fchi-cubic}
      \Fchi_{\mu\nu}(s) = s\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} + s^3\, \mathcal{R}_{\chi,\mu\nu} + O(s^5), \qquad
      \mathcal{R}_{\chi,\mu\nu} := \tfrac{1}{3!}\, \partial_s^3 \Fchi_{\mu\nu}\big|_{s=0} ,
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is the cubic response of the chiral two-form in the chart $s$.
    We write $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi} := \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\,\mu\nu}$ for the contraction.

    \begin{proposition}[Quartic of the chiral radicand]
      \label{prop:quartic}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      With the determinantal identity~\eqref{eq:det-expansion} and the odd expansion~\eqref{eq:Fchi-cubic},
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:Dchi-quartic}
        \Dchi(s) = 1 + \frac{1}{2\beta^2}\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu}\, s^2
        + \left( \frac{\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}}{\beta^2}
            - \frac{\big(\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu}\big)^2}{16\beta^4} \right) s^4 + O(s^6) .
      \end{equation}
      The pseudoscalar term is non-positive and fixed by $\Pchi$ alone; the coefficient $\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}$
      depends on the chart (Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}).
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The exact four-dimensional identity~\eqref{eq:det-expansion} is quadratic in the two invariants
      $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \Fchi^{\mu\nu}$ and $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Fchi}^{\mu\nu}$.
      Substituting~\eqref{eq:Fchi-cubic}, the scalar invariant is
      $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \Fchi^{\mu\nu} = s^2\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} + 2 s^4\, \Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}
      + O(s^6)$, and the pseudoscalar invariant is
      $\Fchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Fchi}^{\mu\nu} = s^2\, \Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu} + O(s^4)$,
      so its square is $O(s^4)$.
      Collecting the $s^4$ coefficients gives~\eqref{eq:Dchi-quartic}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{lemma}[Parametrisation dependence of the quartic coefficient]
      \label{lem:gauge}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, and $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}\neq0$.
      Under an odd reparametrisation $s=t+\alpha t^3+O(t^5)$, the two-form $\hat\Fchi(t)=\Fchi(s(t))$ has the same
      $\Pchi$ and the cubic response $\hat{\mathcal R}_\chi=\mathcal R_\chi+\alpha\Pchi$, so that
      $\Pchi\cdot\hat{\mathcal R}_\chi=\Pchi\cdot\mathcal R_\chi+\alpha\,\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}$, and the
      coefficient
      of $t^4$ in $\Dchi$ shifts by $\alpha\,\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}/\beta^2$, the pseudoscalar coefficient being
      unchanged.
      If $\Pchi_{\mu\nu}\Pchi^{\mu\nu}\neq0$, the coefficient of the quartic term can therefore be given any real value
      by
      the choice of $\alpha$, and has no intrinsic meaning.
      The component $\mathcal R_{\chi,\perp}:=\mathcal R_\chi-(\Pchi\cdot\mathcal R_\chi/\Pchi\cdot\Pchi)\,\Pchi$ is
      invariant: $\hat{\mathcal R}_{\chi,\perp}=\mathcal R_{\chi,\perp}$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      Substituting $s=t+\alpha t^3$ in~\eqref{eq:Fchi-cubic} gives $\hat\Fchi(t)=t\Pchi+t^3(\mathcal R_\chi+\alpha\Pchi)
      +O(t^5)$.
      The contraction shifts by $\alpha\Pchi\cdot\Pchi$, and~\eqref{eq:Dchi-quartic} then shifts as stated.
      The projection orthogonal to $\Pchi$ for the Lorentzian pairing removes the $\alpha\Pchi$ term.
    \end{proof}

    For a supplied purely electric two-form (Lemma~\ref{lem:index-assignment}) the pseudoscalar term vanishes:
    $\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P} = 0$ and
    $\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \widetilde{\Pchi}^{\mu\nu} \propto \mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P} \cdot \mathbf{B}_{\mathcal P} = 0$.
    With $\Pchi_{\mu\nu} \Pchi^{\mu\nu} = -2|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2$ the radicand reads, in the chart $s$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Dchi-electric}
      \Dchi(s) = 1 - \frac{|\mathbf{E}_{\mathcal P}|^2}{\beta^2}\, s^2
      + \frac{\Pchi \cdot \mathcal{R}_{\chi}}{\beta^2}\, s^4 + O(s^6) ,
    \end{equation}
    where, by Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}, the quartic coefficient is a property of the chart.

    \begin{proposition}[The quartic selection is chart-dependent]
      \label{prop:amplitude-dichotomy}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, and a supplied purely electric two-form.
      The statements that the quartic truncation of~\eqref{eq:Dchi-electric} has an interior minimum (when
      $\Pchi\cdot\mathcal R_\chi>0$) or none (when $\Pchi\cdot\mathcal R_\chi\le0$) depend on the chart: for every
      real value $c$ there is an odd reparametrisation in which the quartic coefficient equals $c/\beta^2$.
      In particular there is a chart in which the quartic coefficient vanishes, and no quartic stabilisation exists
      there.
      The quartic truncation therefore selects neither a boundary contact nor an interior stationary point of the
      radicand.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} with $\Pchi\cdot\Pchi=-2|\mathbf E_{\mathcal P}|^2\neq0$, the shift
      $\alpha\Pchi\cdot\Pchi/\beta^2$ takes every real value as $\alpha$ varies.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Collinear electric ray]
      \label{cor:radial-contact}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian} and~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, with $F_0$ purely electric,
      $\mathbf B_{F_0}=0$.
      Then $\Dchi(s)=1-a\,\varphi(s)^2$ exactly, with $a:=|\mathbf{E}_{F_0}|^2/\beta^2$.
      A contact $\Dchi(s_*)=0$ exists if and only if $|\varphi|$ attains $1/\sqrt a=\beta/|\mathbf{E}_{F_0}|$, and the
      first
      contact is the first such $s_*>0$.
      In a chart $t=\varphi(s)$ that is increasing on $[0,s_*]$ the contact is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:s-star-affine}
        t_* = \frac{\beta}{|\mathbf{E}_{F_0}|} .
      \end{equation}
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      For $\Fchi=\varphi F_0$ with $\mathbf{B}_{F_0}=0$ the pseudoscalar invariant vanishes identically and the
      identity~\eqref{eq:det-expansion}, which is exact, gives
      $\Dchi=1+\varphi^2F_{0\,\mu\nu}F_0^{\mu\nu}/(2\beta^2)=1-a\varphi^2$.
    \end{proof}

    The contact is thus determined by the values of the odd function $\varphi$ and exists only if $\varphi$ reaches the
    value $\beta/|\mathbf{E}_{F_0}|$; the corpus supplies no bound on $\varphi$.
    The value $t_*$ in the chart $t=\varphi(s)$ is exact; its position in the Schur modulus $s$ is not determined.

    \begin{remark}[No quartic Landau closure]
      \label{rem:not-landau}
      A quartic double well in the chart $s$ would be an artefact of the chart
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy}).
      The amplitude is a bounded-flux problem, not a polynomial minimisation, and the magnitude $|u|$ follows from a
      contact value only after the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ maps the modulus to the
      split through the odd carrier $u(s)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}.
    \end{remark}

    The cubic response is itself a transported Schur datum.
    Since the Schur form $\Epi = -\Pi_S D (1 - P) D \Pi_S^*$ is linear in $P$ at fixed $D, \Pi_S$~\cite{Beau2026prs},
    the transport acts term by term on the modulus expansion, so, under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form},
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:Rchi-transport}
      \mathcal{R}_{\chi} = \tfrac{1}{3!}\, \iota\Big(\Pi_S D \big(\partial_s^3 P|_{0}\big) D \Pi_S^*
      \big|_{\mathrm{out},\,\JPi\text{-odd}}\Big) ,
    \end{equation}
    the same transport as the polarisation~\eqref{eq:Pchi-schur} applied to the third-order tilt.
    If the locking operators $P(s)$ are projectors, $P(s)^2=P(s)$ (a stronger assumption than $0\preceq P(s)\preceq1$),
    the constraint fixes only the $P_0$-diagonal part of each derivative in terms of lower ones; the off-diagonal cubic
    part is unconstrained.
    Throughout this note $P$ denotes the locking operator of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, not $\Pi_S^*\Pi_S$; the two coincide
    only at $s=0$.
    Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, $\mathcal R_\chi$ is parallel to $\Pchi$ and $\mathcal R_{\chi,\perp}=0$
    (Remark~\ref{rem:collinear}).

    \begin{hypothesis}[Fixed-generator Lorentzian projector orbit]
      \label{hyp:fixed-generator-orbit}
      The Lorentzian complexification of the eliminated spinorial block is realised, to the order relevant for the
      chiral amplitude, by a fixed generator $X$,
      \[
        P(s) = e^{sX} P_0 e^{-sX} .
      \]
      Equivalently, the projector path carries no torsion term in the opened $\JPi$-odd direction up to cubic order.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{remark}[Conditional character of the backreaction]
      \label{rem:conditional-sign}
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:fixed-generator-orbit} is not derived from the present corpus: the complexification fixes
      the tangent direction $X$, not the orbit.
      Under it, $\partial_s^3 P|_{0} = \operatorname{ad}_X^2(\partial_s P|_{0})$.
      If $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is parallel to $\Pchi$, it is pure reparametrisation gauge, removable by the odd modulus
      transformation $\mathcal{R}_{\chi} \mapsto \mathcal{R}_{\chi} + \alpha \Pchi$, and the trajectory is straight to
      order $s^3$ in the corresponding chart, with no quartic term in $\Dchi$ for a purely electric two-form
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}).
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} the transverse component $\mathcal{R}_{\chi,\perp}$ vanishes in any
      case (Remark~\ref{rem:collinear}).
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Weights and the cascade generator]
      \label{rem:path-invariant}
      The carrier of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} sees only the $\JPi$-odd Hermitian part of the transported
      tilt
      on the outer weights, which is a multiple of $\Jthree$; a real $\JPi$-odd off-diagonal block on the outer weights
      is
      anti-Hermitian and is not seen.
      For the real generator $X=\log g$, $g=\exp(E)\exp(2F)$, lifted to $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ in the basis $(e_0,e_+,e_-)$,
      and the matrix $P_0=\diag(0.3,1,0.7)$, the outer block of $\operatorname{ad}_X^3P_0$ has a non-zero $\JPi$-odd
      off-diagonal part.
      This is a computation on matrices: $P_0$ is not $\JPi$-invariant and $e^{sX}P_0e^{-sX}$ is not self-adjoint, so
      the example is not an instance of the premises of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}.
      No conclusion is drawn about a cubic transverse channel of the locking trajectory.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Chart covariance of the defect rate and of the first contact}
    \label{sec:boundary}

    The companion eliminated-block note~\cite{Beau2026ebj} proves, in the chiral frame with
    $\JPi = \Sigma_\tau \circ \mathrm{conj}$, $\Sigma_\tau = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & \tau \\ \tau^{-1} & 0
    \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\tau : \mathcal{S}_R \to \mathcal{S}_L$ the unitary intertwiner of~\cite{Beau2026prs}
    with $\tau\,\overline{\tau}^{-1} = \pm 1$, that the left-handed block of the $\JPi$-odd tangent
    of the eliminated block is half the transported equivariance defect rate $\partial_s \Dchi(P)|_0$, with
    $\Dchi(P) = \pi_{LL} - \tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$; this determines one block of the split-sourcing
    tangent, not a unique carrier, the selection of the family and the localisation of the odd tangent onto that block
    remaining open there.
    Throughout this section $\Dchi(P)$ is this equivariance defect, its first-order coefficient being the defect rate,
    while $\Dchi(s)$ is the admissibility radicand of Definition~\ref{def:B}: the two share the chiral subscript but
    are distinct objects, distinguished by their argument.

    \begin{proposition}[The defect rate is reparametrisation-covariant]
      \label{prop:rate-covariant}
      Assume the finite-locking equivariance $\Dchi(P(0))=0$ of~\cite{Beau2026prs}.
      Under a reparametrisation of the modulus $s \mapsto \psi(s)$, $\psi(0) = 0$, $\psi'(0) \neq 0$, the defect
      rate is covariant, not invariant: it rescales by the Jacobian $\psi'(0)$.
      Hence the rate alone carries no chart-independent magnitude.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      $\Dchi(P)$ is a function on the modulus line; the chain rule at $s = 0$, with $\psi(0) = 0$ and
      $\Dchi(P(0)) = 0$, makes the first-order coefficient scale by $\psi'(0)$ between charts.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[Chart-independence of the first contact]
      \label{prop:contact-invariant}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:lorentzian}, \ref{hyp:chiral-two-form} and~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}.
      The first contact, the first $s_*>0$ with $\Dchi(s_*)=0$, defines a point $\Fchi(s_*)$ of the trajectory
      $\{\Fchi(s)\}$ that is independent of the chart, for increasing charts on $[0,s_*]$ (a decreasing chart sends it
      to the conjugate
      point $-\Fchi(s_*)$); the parameter value
      $s_*$ itself transforms with the chart.
      The value $u(s_*)$ at that point is invariant under orientation-preserving reparametrisation, and $|u(s_*)|$
      under every odd reparametrisation, the sign labelling the branch.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The contact is the level condition $\Dchi=0$ on the trajectory, a point of the curve, carried by any
      reparametrisation to the same configuration.
      An orientation-reversing chart exchanges the two members $\pm s_*$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:u-odd}, flips the sign
      of $u$ while preserving $|u(s_*)|$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[No selection of the A4 lock]
      \label{cor:boundary-admissible}
      Nothing in this note selects the saturation contact over an interior stationary point of the radicand as the A4
      lock.
      The quartic coefficient that could distinguish them is chart-dependent
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:amplitude-dichotomy}),
      Remark~\ref{rem:collinear} makes the trajectory collinear under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-two-form}, and
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:local-min} does not distinguish a boundary
      contact from an interior minimum.
      The existence of a locked pair is the content of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}.
      The magnitude $|u|=|u(s_*)|$ then involves the value of the split at the contact, sourced at first order by the
      $\JPi$-odd tangent of the eliminated block together with the first-order motion of the spinorial
      embedding~\cite{Beau2026ebj}.
    \end{remark}

    \paragraph{Front D factorisation (conditional).}
      Under the localisation hypothesis that the first-order source of the split is exhausted by the transported
      left-handed block $\tfrac12 \partial_s \Dchi(P)|_0$ of the $\JPi$-odd tangent, that is, that the $J_3$ component
      of the first-order residue $E_1$ of~\cite{Beau2026ebj} coincides with that of the auxiliary compression
      $E_1^{\mathrm{aux}} = -G_0\,\dot Q(0)\,G_0^{*}$, $G_0 = \Pi_S(0)\,D$ the finite embedding composed with the Dirac
      operator, the two embedding-motion terms contributing nothing to it, and that within the compression the
      chirally off-diagonal part of the odd tangent contributes nothing either, the contact split
      $u_\partial = u(s_*)$ of a covariant family factorises, to first order in $s_*$ (the linear regime of the odd
      carrier, which is not controlled at the contact), into three separated normalisations:
      \[
        u_\partial = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma\,s_*\,\frac{T_{J_3}\bigl(\partial_s \Dchi(P)|_0\bigr)}{\langle J_3, J_3\rangle}.
      \]
      Here $\sqrt{2}\,\sigma$ is the even-block factor, from the algebraic value
      $E_0^2|_{\Cgen} = \diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, with $\sigma = \pm1$ the branch sign;
      $s_*$ is the first contact $\Dchi(s_*) = 0$ when it exists, carrying the dependence on the trajectory of the
      two-form; and $T_{J_3}$ is the oriented Cartan projection
      of the $\mathcal{D}^{\pm}$-transported chiral defect rate, the angular symplectic-area observable of the
      oriented-frontier thread --- a closed linear functional evaluated on the defect rate, the factor one half of the
      block identity being absorbed in its normalisation, not a positive norm --- whose normalised evaluation is
      governed by the dictionary-bound frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$.
      The hypothesis is not established in~\cite{Beau2026ebj}, which determines one block of the odd tangent, not a
      unique carrier, and whose covariant realisation has a split rate sourced also by the first-order motion of the
      embedding and different from the rate of the auxiliary compression, which is not the residue, so that the
      hypothesis fails in that family and can hold only as a property of the family selected by the programme; the
      selection of the family and the localisation of the odd tangent onto its left-handed block remain open there.
      Under the hypothesis the remaining magnitude data are the two upstream quantities already isolated --- the radial
      contact $s_*$ through the A4 radicand curvature, and the angular normalisation of $T_{J_3}$ through
      $\mathcal{N}_A$ --- so the numerical magnitude remains upstream and dictionary-bound.

    \paragraph{Radial contact (Front 1).}
      For a purely electric $F_0$, $\Dchi=1-a\varphi(s)^2$ exactly and the contact is $t_*=\beta/|\mathbf{E}_{F_0}|$ in
      the
      chart $t=\varphi(s)$ when $|\varphi|$ reaches that value (Corollary~\ref{cor:radial-contact}); its position in the

    \paragraph{Open.}
      The magnitude $|u|$ is not determined.
      The open data are the discrete chiral-frontier normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ that maps the locked modulus to
      $|u|$, the existence of a locked pair (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:nondegenerate}), the selection of the lock between a
      boundary contact and an interior stationary point, and the function $\varphi$ that fixes the position of the
      contact.
      The cubic response is understood modulo odd reparametrisation of the local modulus: under
      $s = t + \alpha t^3 + O(t^5)$ one has
      \[
        \mathcal{R}_{\chi} \mapsto \mathcal{R}_{\chi} + \alpha\, \Pchi ,
      \]
      so the $\Pchi$-parallel component of $\mathcal{R}_{\chi}$ is a parametrisation gauge.
      This is downstream of the genus and is not a genus question: the genus is not determined here
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:index-assignment}).
      In the Schur-null locus the polarisation vanishes and the locking character falls to the marginal case of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}, decided by the leading even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$.

      The normalisation $\mathcal{N}_A$ is itself dictionary-bound, not a parameter-free prediction.
      It is the angular analogue of the radial scale $\kappa = 5/12$ of the exit-deficit dictionary~\cite{Beau2026aar,
    Beau2026q14}, which fixes the split value $\varepsilon = 1/10$ by matching the derived three-level deficit structure
      to the external ADE ratio $3{:}2$ of a selected case, one of four three-level cases of differing
      ratios~\cite{Beau2026a4}.
      The first-principles status of $\varepsilon = 1/10$ is therefore exactly that of the ADE case-selection gate, and
      for the present corpus construction that gate is a no-go: its obstruction is isolated in
      AOG-Note~\cite{Beau2026aog} as an arithmetic orthogonality between Born--Infeld parity and the Galois-spin action,
      established by a character-table audit of $2I \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,5)$.
      The residual frequency reflection $R_b = W(-I)$ is the central Weil lift, and its commutation with $\gamma_5$
      requires a Weil-to-spin group map that no source supplies~\cite{Beau2026q14}; under that map the chiral oriented
      signal that would carry $\mathcal{N}_A$ is defined and its integrand survives the symmetric-frontier cancellation
      that annihilates the bare cocycle~\cite{Beau2026q11of, Beau2026aar}; but evaluating it and comparing to
      $\varepsilon = 1/10$ would be a dictionary matching, not a test,
      since $1/10$ is a dictionary value and $\mathcal{N}_A$ its conversion factor.
      The genuine first-principles front is therefore upstream and structural, not a numerical amplitude: the ADE case
      selection and the level-to-generation map, the derivation of the projective-resolution growth
      $\Lambda_{\mathrm{proj}}(n)$ and of the cascade exponent $\beta$ (the central open problem
      of~\cite{Beau2026a4}; no structural bound on $\beta$ is established, the Born--Infeld/Ramanujan route not
      yielding one~\cite{Beau2026a8}), and the derivation of the transfer constant $N_{\mathrm{casc}}$ that
      fixes $\mathcal{N}_A$~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
